# Creativity and everyday-skills expansion review

Source SHA-256: `1fe51da9ad19a26950c55ec66499142905354c8bada73194aaec8c64f3126168`

Method: AI author self-review of all 120 new activities, followed by the specific corrections from independent AI review of all four age bands relayed by the integrating agent. A separate AI reviewer also completed the learning-objective and metadata pass across all 120 entries; its two wording findings were corrected and verified before the final source freeze. This is not a human, teacher, child-development, or native-speaker certification. No sessions with children were observed or conducted.

## Coverage and validation

- 120 original additions: 60 creativity and 60 everyday-skills/cooperation activities.
- Exactly 15 additions for each age/area combination across ages 3–4, 5–6, 7–8, and 9–12; IDs end in `06` through `20`.
- Every entry follows the existing full bilingual schema, with a concrete question, explained example answer, hint, three steps, materials, preparation, participation alternative, supervision note, and easier/harder variations.
- All 120 have individually written learning objectives in both languages. The questions cover different problems rather than changing names or numbers within one universal exercise.
- Validated exact schema keys against an original activity, accepted enums and durations, complete English/Arabic text, equal bilingual array lengths, three steps, nonempty hints and materials, example-answer labels, and expected IDs/counts.
- Compared normalized titles and prompts with all 480 saved activities: no duplicate English or Arabic title or prompt was found. Each of the 120 additions also has a distinct objective in each language.
- Each activity uses paper and a drawing tool; all physical characters, devices, printing situations, meeting plans, and other equipment mentioned in questions are imagined scenarios. The younger groups specify large crayons and adult-supported reading; the older groups specify a pencil and permit adult reading or acting help. Durations are activity estimates, not performance requirements.

## Editorial checks

Reviewed the question, proposed answer, explanation, hint, action, and variations of all 120 for internal consistency, meaningful differences, age guidance, material feasibility, and English/Arabic equivalence. Open-ended answers are labelled as examples and allow other reasoned approaches. Personal taste is not treated as a correctness criterion. The questions include drawing, verbal explanation, optional gesture or gentle sound, and fictional role play; the alternatives permit a partner to draw or write.

Checked explicit constraints such as three shapes, task order, resource quantities, and time estimates. The final older questions distinguish uncertain estimates from promises, observations from assumed motives, limited evidence from universal conclusions, and private sharing from permission to display an idea. These are fictional learning prompts, not medical, legal, or parenting advice.

The expressive-lettering activity is usable in the selected language: English uses “stretch” and Arabic uses «تمدد». Arabic letter joining and readability are preserved, and comparison with the other language is optional. Other Arabic adaptations preserve the task's purpose and relevant details without requiring knowledge of both languages.

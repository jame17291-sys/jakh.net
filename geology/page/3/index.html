41What is the Wilson Cycle in plate tectonics?

Answer

The Wilson Cycle describes the periodic opening and closing of ocean basins over hundreds of millions of years. Continents rift apart, an ocean forms, then subduction closes the ocean and continents collide, forming mountains — after which rifting can begin again.

42What is an ophiolite and why is it geologically significant?

Answer

An ophiolite is a section of oceanic crust and upper mantle that has been thrust onto continental crust during subduction. They are significant because they provide geologists with direct access to material from the ocean floor and upper mantle.

43What is the concept of 'uniformitarianism' proposed by James Hutton?

Answer

Uniformitarianism is the principle that the same natural processes operating today (erosion, deposition, volcanism) also operated in the past at roughly the same rates. 'The present is the key to the past.'

44What is a pyroclastic flow and why is it considered the deadliest volcanic hazard?

Answer

A pyroclastic flow is a fast-moving current of hot gas, ash, and volcanic rock that travels down a volcano's slopes at up to 700 km/h at temperatures exceeding 1000°C. It is deadly because it moves too fast to outrun and incinerates everything in its path.

45What is mantle convection and how does it drive plate tectonics?

Answer

Mantle convection is the slow circulation of hot solid rock in the mantle driven by heat from the Earth's core. Hot material rises, spreads, cools, and sinks, dragging tectonic plates along with it — causing both seafloor spreading at ridges and subduction at trenches.

46What caused the mass extinction at the end of the Cretaceous period approximately 66 million years ago?

Answer

A combination of a massive asteroid impact (Chicxulub, Yucatán) and prolonged volcanic eruptions (Deccan Traps, India), which caused global cooling and eliminated about 75% of all species including non-avian dinosaurs.

47What are Love waves and Rayleigh waves?

Answer

They are surface seismic waves. Love waves cause horizontal shaking perpendicular to the direction of travel. Rayleigh waves cause a rolling elliptical motion at the surface. Both are slower than body waves (P and S) but cause most of the damage during earthquakes.

48What is the difference between partial melting and fractional crystallization in magma genesis?

Answer

Partial melting occurs when rock in the mantle or crust melts partially, with different minerals melting at different temperatures — producing magma of a different composition from the source rock. Fractional crystallization occurs as magma cools, with minerals crystallizing out in sequence, progressively changing the composition of the remaining melt.

49What is the geomagnetic reversal and how is it recorded in rocks?

Answer

A geomagnetic reversal is when Earth's magnetic north and south poles swap positions. It is recorded in igneous rocks: as magma cools, magnetic minerals align with the current field direction and are locked in place. Alternating stripes of normal and reversed polarity on the ocean floor were key evidence for seafloor spreading.

50What is the principle of superposition in stratigraphy?

Answer

In an undisturbed sequence of sedimentary rock layers, the oldest layers are at the bottom and the youngest are at the top. This principle, formulated by Nicolas Steno in the 17th century, is the foundation of relative dating in geology.

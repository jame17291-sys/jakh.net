01Using the numbers 2, 3 and 4 exactly once, and only addition and multiplication, how can you make 14?

Answer

3 × 4 + 2 = 14, or 2 × (3 + 4) = 14.

02A two-digit number has digits that add to 11. Reversing its digits makes the number 27 greater. What is the original number?

Answer

47. If the tens digit is a and the ones digit is b, then a+b=11 and 9(b−a)=27, so b−a=3. Thus a=4 and b=7.

06Exactly two of four lamps A, B, C, and D are lit. A and B are in opposite states, C is in the same state as A, and D is off. Which lamps are lit?

Answer

A and C. If A were off, only B would be lit, contradicting the condition that exactly two lamps are lit. Therefore A and C are on, while B and D are off.

10On an island, every person is either a knight (always tells truth) or a knave (always lies). You meet two people, A and B. A says: 'At least one of us is a knave.' What are A and B?

Answer

A is a knight and B is a knave. If A were a knave, his statement ('at least one is a knave') would be true — a contradiction. So A is a knight telling the truth, which means B must be the knave.

11In an idealized two-child model, each child is independently equally likely to be a boy or girl. A family is selected at random from those with at least one boy, without inspecting a particular child. What is the probability of two boys?

Answer

1/3. The equally likely ordered possibilities remaining are boy-boy, boy-girl and girl-boy.

13You have a 4-minute hourglass and a 7-minute hourglass. How can you measure exactly 9 minutes?

Answer

Start both; turn the 4 over at minute 4 and the 7 at minute 7. When the 4 empties again (minute 8), turn the 7 over: its last minute of sand ends at minute 9.

Answer

042. Clue 738 removes 7, 3, and 8. Clue 780 then places 0 in the code but not third. Clues 682 and 614 eliminate 6 and put 2 third. Clue 206 puts 0 first, leaving 4 in the middle.

27One of 1,000 juice bottles has gone bad. A test strip changes colour a day after touching even a drop of the bad juice, and one strip can take drops from many bottles. With just one day, what is the fewest strips you need to find the bad bottle?

Answer

10. Number the bottles in binary and give strip k a drop from every bottle whose k-th binary digit is 1. The strips that change spell out the bad bottle's number (2¹⁰ = 1,024).

28Twelve balls are labelled 1–12. Exactly one has a different mass; the other eleven have the same mass. You do not know whether the odd ball is heavier or lighter. Give three balance-scale weighings and a complete rule to identify the odd ball and its type.

Answer

Weigh left versus right: (1) 5,7,8,9 vs 6,10,11,12; (2) 2,3,4,6 vs 5,10,11,12; (3) 1,4,9,10 vs 2,6,7,12. Record L if left is heavier, R if right is heavier, and = if balanced. Heavy-ball signatures: 1:==L; 2:=LR; 3:=L=; 4:=LL; 5:LR=; 6:RLR; 7:L=R; 8:L==; 9:L=L; 10:RRL; 11:RR=; 12:RRR. A matching signature identifies a heavy ball. Otherwise swap L and R in your result; the matching signature identifies a light ball. All 24 signatures are different.

29Ten people stand in a line, each wearing a red or black hat, and each can see only the hats in front. Starting from the back, each says one colour, trying to name their own hat. If they agree on a plan beforehand, how many can be sure of being right?

Answer

9. The person at the back says 'red' if he sees an odd number of red hats, otherwise 'black'. Everyone else can then work out their own colour; only he takes a 50–50 chance.

30100 prisoners may agree on a plan before isolation. A lamp starts off. One prisoner visits its room at a time and may change the lamp. Everyone is selected repeatedly without limit. How can one prisoner eventually know with certainty that all have visited?

Answer

Choose one counter. Each other prisoner signals once: on the first visit when they have not yet signalled and find the lamp off, they turn it on. On all other visits they leave it unchanged. Only the counter turns it off, counting each such event. At 99 signals, all have visited. Repeated selection guarantees eventual completion.

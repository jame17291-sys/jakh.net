21What is the Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse (1940) famous for in engineering?

Answer

It demonstrated aeroelastic flutter — resonance induced by wind caused the bridge to oscillate and destroy itself, fundamentally changing aerodynamic design requirements for bridges.

22What is the difference between potable water and grey water?

Answer

Potable water is treated, clean water safe for consumption. Grey water is lightly used wastewater from sinks, showers, and laundry — reusable for irrigation but not safe to drink without further treatment.

23Why is electricity transmitted at very high voltages over long distances?

Answer

Higher voltage means lower current for the same power, and lower current means much less energy lost as heat in transmission line resistance (P = I²R). Voltages are then stepped down locally for safe use.

24What engineering challenge made the Channel Tunnel remarkable?

Answer

25What is a pile foundation and when is it used?

Answer

Long columns driven deep into the ground to transfer structural loads to deep, stronger soil or rock. Used when surface soil is too weak to support a building's weight directly.

Answer

27What is combined sewer overflow (CSO) and why is it an environmental problem?

Answer

A combined sewer carries both sewage and storm water. During heavy rain, the system overflows, discharging untreated sewage mixed with rainwater into rivers or the sea, contaminating them with pathogens.

28What is the difference between a simply supported beam bridge and a continuous beam bridge?

Answer

A simply supported beam spans between two supports independently. A continuous beam extends over multiple supports, distributing loads more efficiently but requiring foundations that resist upward reaction forces.

29What is the water hammer effect in pipelines?

Answer

A pressure surge caused by sudden change in water flow velocity — such as rapid valve closure. It can damage pipes and valves. Engineers mitigate it using surge tanks and slow-closing valves.

30What is the difference between AC and DC in power transmission?

Answer

AC reverses direction periodically and is easily transformed to different voltages, ideal for grid transmission. DC flows constantly in one direction and is more efficient for very long distance (HVDC) or submarine cable transmission.

34What is a bascule (drawbridge) and how does it work?

Answer

A movable bridge section that pivots upward like a seesaw to let tall ships pass. A counterweight on one end reduces the force needed to raise the movable span.

35How does Bernoulli's principle apply in pipeline engineering?

Answer

Increasing fluid velocity decreases pressure. Engineers use this to design venturi flow meters and predict pressure changes at pipe constrictions, helping size pumps and prevent cavitation.

36What is the difference between a metro (subway) and a light rail system?

Answer

A metro runs on fully grade-separated, dedicated track — usually underground or elevated — with high frequency and capacity. Light rail may share road space, runs at surface level, carries fewer passengers, but costs less to build.

38What is the Instrument Landing System (ILS) used at airports?

Answer

39What is the California Bearing Ratio (CBR) test used for in road construction?

Answer

It measures a soil's load-bearing capacity by comparing the pressure to penetrate a soil sample versus a standard crushed rock, determining whether ground can support pavement layers.

40What is structural redundancy and why is it important in infrastructure?

Answer

Designing systems with multiple load paths so failure of one element does not cause total collapse. A redundant bridge tolerates one member failing; a redundant grid reroutes power around failed lines.

41What is a cascading failure in power grid infrastructure?

Answer

An initial failure overloads adjacent elements, causing them to fail in turn — a domino effect. The 2003 US Northeast blackout cut power to 55 million people this way.

42What is induced demand in transportation planning?

Answer

Building more road capacity generates additional traffic rather than relieving congestion — the increased ease of travel attracts more drivers until the new capacity fills up and congestion returns.

43What is non-revenue water (NRW) and why does it matter for utilities?

Answer

The difference between water put into a distribution system and water billed to customers — including leaks, pipe bursts, meter errors, and theft. In many cities NRW exceeds 30% of production, representing significant revenue loss and resource waste.

44What is structural health monitoring (SHM) and how is it applied to bridges?

Answer

Networks of embedded sensors (strain gauges, accelerometers, corrosion monitors) continuously measure a structure's response to loads and environment, enabling early detection of damage or degradation before costly failures occur.

45What are pumped-storage hydropower plants and why are they valuable to power grids?

Answer

They pump water uphill during excess electricity periods, then release it through turbines during peak demand — acting as large-scale rechargeable batteries, the most mature form of grid-scale energy storage.

46What is perpetual pavement design and how does it differ from conventional flexible pavement?

Answer

Perpetual pavement is designed so fatigue cracking and rutting remain confined to replaceable surface layers while the deep structural layers last indefinitely. Conventional flexible pavement targets a finite 20-year life before full reconstruction.

47What is the difference between a dry bulk terminal and a liquid bulk terminal in port design?

Answer

Dry bulk handles loose solids (coal, grain) using conveyors and grab cranes. Liquid bulk handles petroleum, chemicals, or LNG using pipelines, pumps, and tanker berths with spill containment. Each requires completely different handling infrastructure.

48What is the 'water-energy nexus' in infrastructure planning?

Answer

The deep interdependency between water and energy: energy is needed to pump, treat, and distribute water; water is needed for power plant cooling, hydropower, and fuel extraction. Stressing one resource typically stresses the other.

49What is 'service life design' in infrastructure and how does it differ from prescriptive design?

Answer

Service life design specifies a target lifespan (e.g. 100 years) and selects materials and protective measures to achieve it. Prescriptive design follows code rules without explicitly targeting a lifespan — it is less precise for long-term asset management.

50What is critical infrastructure interdependency and why does it complicate resilience planning?

Answer

Power, water, transport, and telecoms systems are deeply interdependent — power outages disable water pumps; telecom failures disrupt emergency response; transport breakdowns prevent maintenance. Restoring one system often requires another to already work, creating cascading failure risks that require holistic resilience modelling.

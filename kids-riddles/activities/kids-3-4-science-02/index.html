Kids Learning & Play / Float or sink tray

Ages 3–4 · Science & nature

Float or sink tray

Test large everyday objects in shallow water.

15 minRead with a grown-upPlay together

What we’re practising

Make a prediction and compare it with an observation.

Before you begin

An adult puts the tray on a towel and adds only enough water for the test objects to float freely without touching the bottom.

Let’s try it

  1. Predict what the wooden spoon will do, then lower it gently.
  2. Try the metal spoon and describe where each rests.
  3. Try the empty cup upright, then tip water into it and observe any change.

Talk about it

Did everything behave as you expected?

A little hint
  • Look at whether the object stays at the surface or rests below it.
Answer & what to notice

Record what happens: a wooden spoon often floats and a metal spoon usually sinks; cup behaviour depends on its material and trapped air.

Material, shape and trapped air can all affect floating; size alone does not decide it.

Make it easier

Test just the two spoons.

Take it further

Test the cup again and explain what changed.

Away from the screen

Notice floating objects in a picture book.

What you’ll need

  • Wide shallow tray
  • Water
  • Large wooden spoon
  • Large metal spoon
  • Unbreakable empty cup
  • Towel

Use what you have

Use two different large bath toys if spoons are unavailable.

Grown-up note

An adult stays within reach throughout, prevents drinking, wipes spills and empties the tray immediately afterwards.

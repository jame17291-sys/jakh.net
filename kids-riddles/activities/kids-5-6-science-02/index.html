Ages 5–6 · Science & nature

Which cloth drinks water?

Compare how two materials take up the same small splash.

15 minRead with a grown-upPlay together

What we’re practising

Observe absorption while keeping starting conditions similar.

Before you begin

Put two dry surfaces on a tray; an adult measures the water.

Let’s try it

  1. Predict which surface will soak up water.
  2. Put one teaspoon of water on each surface and wait a minute.
  3. Look for spreading dampness or a separate puddle, then discuss a useful cleaning material.

Talk about it

Which material would you choose for a small spill?

A little hint
  • Check whether the water stays separate or enters the material.
Answer & what to notice

Cotton usually absorbs water; smooth plastic keeps it on the surface. Coatings can change results.

Absorbent materials take water into small spaces within them.

Make it easier

Observe one drop-sized splash on each surface with adult help.

Take it further

Repeat with dry samples and two equal spoonfuls each.

Away from the screen

Choose a suitable cloth to help clean the tray.

What you’ll need

  • Cotton cloth
  • Smooth plastic lid
  • Tray
  • Teaspoon
  • Small cup of water

Use what you have

Compare paper towel with an unbreakable plate.

Grown-up note

An adult supervises water, keeps it from electrical items, wipes spills and empties the cup afterwards.

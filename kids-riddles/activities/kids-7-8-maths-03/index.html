Measure twice

Discover why the same table has different measurements in different units.

15 minIndependent readerGrown-up nearby

What we’re practising

Measure a length with repeated equal units and explain unit size.

Before you begin

Choose a reachable table edge and label the two measuring units A and B.

Let’s try it

  1. Predict which pencil will need more end-to-end placements.
  2. Move the short pencil along the edge without gaps, counting whole placements and noting any remainder.
  3. Repeat with the longer pencil along the same edge.
  4. Record each result with its unit name.

Talk about it

Did the table change, or did the unit change?

A little hint
  • A smaller unit usually fits more times into a fixed length.
Answer & what to notice

Measurements depend on the objects used; the shorter unit needs at least as many placements.

Equal units placed without gaps make a repeatable measurement; unit names make the number meaningful.

Make it easier

Measure a short book edge using one unit.

Take it further

Compare both measurements with a ruler and discuss leftover fractions.

Away from the screen

Measure three safe objects using one shared unit and order their lengths.

What you’ll need

  • A short pencil
  • A longer pencil
  • Paper

Use what you have

Use two different lengths of folded paper.

Grown-up note

Measure reachable surfaces without climbing; keep pencil points away from faces.

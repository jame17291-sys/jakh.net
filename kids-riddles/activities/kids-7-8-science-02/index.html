Kids Learning & Play / Where does the water go?

Ages 7–8 · Science & nature

Where does the water go?

Compare how three materials respond to the same small amount of water.

15 minIndependent readerPlay together

What we’re practising

Distinguish absorption from water sitting on a surface.

Before you begin

Place dry samples on a tray away from electrical devices.

Let’s try it

  1. Predict which sample will absorb water.
  2. Put one teaspoon of water on each sample.
  3. Wait about one minute, then look for spreading, soaking or a puddle.
  4. Describe the evidence and dry the tray.

Talk about it

How can you tell soaked-in water from a puddle on top?

A little hint
  • Look for a wet patch spreading through the material rather than a movable puddle.
Answer & what to notice

Cloth and paper towel usually soak up water; a solid plastic lid generally keeps it on the surface.

Many absorbent materials have tiny spaces that hold water; a smooth plastic surface does not soak it up in the same way.

Make it easier

Observe only one absorbent and one non-absorbent sample.

Take it further

Compare equal-sized samples of two cloths, repeating with fresh dry pieces.

Away from the screen

Choose which tested material would help wipe up a small spill and explain why.

What you’ll need

  • Tray
  • Cloth square
  • Paper towel square
  • Plastic lid
  • Teaspoon
  • Water

Use what you have

Compare just a cloth and a plastic lid.

Grown-up note

An adult supervises water use; wipe spills promptly and do not drink test water.

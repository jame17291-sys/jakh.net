Ages 7–8 · Science & nature

Flat or crumpled?

Compare how two equal sheets fall when their shapes differ.

15 minIndependent readerGrown-up nearby

What we’re practising

Test the effect of shape on falling through air.

Before you begin

Clear a floor area and choose a drop height no higher than your shoulder.

Let’s try it

  1. Leave one sheet flat and crumple the other tightly.
  2. Predict which will land first.
  3. Hold them at the same height and release together; repeat three times.
  4. Compare results, including any release that was not simultaneous.

Talk about it

Did crumpling add any paper or change only its shape?

A little hint
  • A wide flat sheet pushes against more air as it falls.
Answer & what to notice

The crumpled sheet usually lands first; uneven release or drafts can affect a trial.

The sheets have the same amount of paper, but shape changes air resistance; mass alone does not explain this result.

Make it easier

Watch an adult release both sheets and describe what happened.

Take it further

Compare loose and tight crumpling while keeping paper size the same.

Away from the screen

Sketch a paper shape that might fall slowly, then test it at the same safe height.

What you’ll need

  • Two identical sheets of scrap paper

Use what you have

Tear one sheet into two equal halves with adult help.

Grown-up note

Stay on the floor, never climb or drop objects from windows, and retrieve paper before walking.

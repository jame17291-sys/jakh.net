A pull through paper

Recognise a non-contact force from evidence.

10 minIndependent readerGrown-up nearby

What we’re practising

Recognise a non-contact force from evidence.

Before you begin

A magnet attracts an iron paper clip with a thin sheet of ordinary paper between them; use a drawing.

Let’s try it

  1. A magnet attracts an iron paper clip with a thin sheet of ordinary paper between them; use a drawing.
  2. Ask: Does a magnetic pull always require the magnet to touch the clip directly?
  3. Choose or draw an answer. Explain it using the clues; check the explanation together after trying.

Talk about it

A magnet attracts an iron paper clip with a thin sheet of ordinary paper between them; use a drawing. Does a magnetic pull always require the magnet to touch the clip directly?

A little hint
  • The paper separates the objects in the example.
Answer & what to notice

No; attraction can act across a small gap and through ordinary paper.

The observation is evidence of a force acting without direct contact.

Make it easier

Point to the separating paper.

Take it further

Predict why a much larger gap might weaken the effect.

Away from the screen

Draw the idea as a two-picture explanation and tell someone which part is observed and which part is explained.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Pencil or crayons

Use what you have

An adult can describe the scene aloud and accept a spoken or pointed answer. No printer is needed.

Grown-up note

Explore the situation on paper with suitable drawing tools. Ask an adult before trying a real-world investigation.

Kids Learning & Play / Invent a five-minute board game

Ages 9–12 · Creativity & making

Invent a five-minute board game

Design a small game, test its rules and fix one confusing moment.

30 minIndependent readerPlay together

What we’re practising

Turn an idea into explicit rules and revise after observing play.

Before you begin

Draw a track of twelve spaces with a start and finish.

Let’s try it

  1. Choose a turn rule, such as move one or two spaces, and an exact winning condition.
  2. Add one optional decision, such as a shortcut that costs the next turn.
  3. Play a short round with a partner and note questions about the rules.
  4. Revise one rule, then play again and compare.

Talk about it

Could a player get stuck forever or be unable to make a legal move?

A little hint
  • Decide what happens if a move would pass the finish before play begins.
Answer & what to notice

Many designs work if players can understand turns, legal moves and how the game ends.

Playtesting reveals unclear rules and unintended loops that are hard to notice in a sketch.

Make it easier

Use a straight track with only one turn rule.

Take it further

Make a cooperative version where both players work toward a shared goal.

Away from the screen

Write a one-page rule sheet and let another pair try without coaching.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Pencil
  • Two large paper counters

Use what you have

Use crossed-out marks instead of movable counters.

Grown-up note

Use large counters, avoid gambling or real prizes, and let players stop freely.

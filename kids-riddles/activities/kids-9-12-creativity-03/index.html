One-sheet standing sculpture

Build a free-standing paper form using folds, then test its stability.

30 minIndependent readerGrown-up nearby

What we’re practising

Explore how shape and base size affect a simple design.

Before you begin

Choose a clear flat table and set the constraint: no tape, glue or cutting.

Let’s try it

  1. Sketch two ways folds might make the sheet stand.
  2. Build one version and check whether it stands for ten seconds.
  3. Change a fold or widen the base, then test again.
  4. Record the design's height and describe the most useful change.

Talk about it

Can a taller sculpture also be stable, and what tradeoff appears?

A little hint
  • A wider base often helps, while using more paper in the base may reduce height.
Answer & what to notice

Many folded forms work; success means standing unaided under the chosen conditions, not reaching a fixed height.

Folds change stiffness and where the paper's weight is supported; the base must support the whole form.

Make it easier

Make a simple tent fold and compare two opening widths.

Take it further

Design for both a minimum height and a maximum base width you choose in advance.

Away from the screen

Draw a labelled museum card explaining the sculpture's construction.

What you’ll need

  • One sheet of paper
  • Pencil
  • Ruler if available

Use what you have

Use two equal half-sheets for two smaller designs.

Grown-up note

Keep models on a low table and avoid standing on furniture to measure or display them.

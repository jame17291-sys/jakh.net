Ages 9–12 · Logic & problem-solving

The river crossing plan

Move three fictional passengers across a river while respecting constraints.

20 minIndependent readerPlay together

What we’re practising

Plan forward and backward moves while checking every intermediate state.

Before you begin

Use a farmer, fox, chicken and grain. The boat carries the farmer plus at most one other item.

Let’s try it

  1. Start all four on the left bank; only the farmer operates the boat.
  2. Do not leave fox with chicken, or chicken with grain, on a bank without the farmer.
  3. Try crossings and record both banks after every move.
  4. Continue until everyone reaches the right bank safely within the puzzle rules.

Talk about it

Why might carrying something back help the plan succeed?

A little hint
  • Take the chicken first; later you will need to bring it back once.
Answer & what to notice

One solution: take chicken across; return alone; take fox across; bring chicken back; take grain across; return alone; take chicken across.

Bringing the chicken back prevents either forbidden pair from being left together without the farmer.

Make it easier

Provide the first two crossings and ask for the next one.

Take it further

Find the alternative solution that transports grain before fox.

Away from the screen

Replace the characters with invented robots that cannot be left in certain pairs.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Pencil
  • Four large paper labels

Use what you have

Draw the riverbanks and write positions instead of moving cards.

Grown-up note

This is a paper logic story only; do not approach water or involve real animals.

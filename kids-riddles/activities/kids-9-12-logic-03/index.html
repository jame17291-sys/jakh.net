The shortest delivery route

Find the least-cost route through a small weighted network.

20 minIndependent readerTry independently

What we’re practising

Compare route totals instead of judging by drawing distance.

Before you begin

Draw points A, B, C, D, E and connect AB=2, AC=5, BC=1, BD=4, CD=1, CE=6, DE=3. All links work both ways.

Let’s try it

  1. Find two routes from A to E and add their labelled costs.
  2. Compare reaching C directly with reaching it through B.
  3. Use the cheaper intermediate choices to find a better route.
  4. Check the final total against the other routes you tried.

Talk about it

Can a route with more links still have a lower total cost?

A little hint
  • A to B to C costs 3, less than the direct A to C cost of 5.
Answer & what to notice

The shortest route is A–B–C–D–E with cost 2+1+1+3=7.

The cheapest costs from A are B=2, C=3, D=4 and E=7; the diagram's drawn distances are irrelevant.

Make it easier

Compare only A–B–D–E and A–B–C–D–E.

Take it further

Close the C–D link and find the equally best routes. Both A–B–D–E and A–B–C–E cost 9.

Away from the screen

Design a five-stop network with two equally cheap routes.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Pencil

Use what you have

Write the possible connections in a table instead of drawing a network.

Grown-up note

All routes are fictional; do not use the drawing for real travel.

The earliest project finish

Find a finish time from dependencies and parallel work.

What we’re practising

Find a finish time from dependencies and parallel work.

Before you begin

Two people can work in parallel: cutting takes 3 minutes, folding 2; assembly takes 4 after both, then drying takes 5.

Let’s try it

  1. Two people can work in parallel: cutting takes 3 minutes, folding 2; assembly takes 4 after both, then drying takes 5.
  2. Ask: Starting both first tasks at minute zero, what is the earliest finish time in this paper schedule?
  3. Choose or draw an answer. Explain it using the clues; check the explanation together after trying.

Talk about it

Two people can work in parallel: cutting takes 3 minutes, folding 2; assembly takes 4 after both, then drying takes 5. Starting both first tasks at minute zero, what is the earliest finish time in this paper schedule?

A little hint
  • Assembly waits for the slower first task.
Answer & what to notice

Minute 12.

Both first tasks are finished at minute 3; assembly ends at 7 and drying at 12.

Make it easier

Draw a timeline for each person.

Take it further

Compare with one person doing cutting and folding sequentially: 14 minutes.

Away from the screen

On another day, draw your own version of this challenge and explain which details must stay the same for the answer to work.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Pencil or crayons

Use what you have

An adult can describe the scene aloud and accept a spoken or pointed answer. No printer is needed.

Grown-up note

Explore the situation on paper with suitable drawing tools. Ask an adult before trying a real-world investigation.

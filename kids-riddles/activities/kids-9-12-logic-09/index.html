Kids Learning & Play / Can three lights all turn on?

Ages 9–12 · Logic & problem-solving

Can three lights all turn on?

Identify an invariant that proves impossibility.

15 minIndependent readerGrown-up nearby

What we’re practising

Identify an invariant that proves impossibility.

Before you begin

Three drawn lights start off. Button A flips lights 1 and 2; B flips 2 and 3; C flips 1 and 3.

Let’s try it

  1. Three drawn lights start off. Button A flips lights 1 and 2; B flips 2 and 3; C flips 1 and 3.
  2. Ask: Can any sequence of presses leave all three lights on? A flip changes on to off or off to on.
  3. Choose or draw an answer. Explain it using the clues; check the explanation together after trying.

Talk about it

Three drawn lights start off. Button A flips lights 1 and 2; B flips 2 and 3; C flips 1 and 3. Can any sequence of presses leave all three lights on? A flip changes on to off or off to on.

A little hint
  • Track whether the number on is even or odd.
Answer & what to notice

No.

Flipping two lights changes the count by plus 2, zero, or minus 2. It stays even, while three is odd.

Make it easier

List the states after one press.

Take it further

Add a button that flips only light 1; find a way to light all three.

Away from the screen

On another day, draw your own version of this challenge and explain which details must stay the same for the answer to work.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Pencil or crayons

Use what you have

An adult can describe the scene aloud and accept a spoken or pointed answer. No printer is needed.

Grown-up note

Explore the situation on paper with suitable drawing tools. Ask an adult before trying a real-world investigation.

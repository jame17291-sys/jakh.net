Scale a colour recipe

Use a ratio to enlarge an imaginary paint mixture without changing its recipe.

15 minIndependent readerTry independently

What we’re practising

Scale both parts of a ratio by the same factor.

Before you begin

Write a pretend recipe: 2 blue scoops and 3 yellow scoops make one batch.

Let’s try it

  1. Draw the five scoops in one batch.
  2. Find how many complete batches make 20 scoops.
  3. Calculate the blue and yellow amounts for that total.
  4. Check that the parts sum to 20 and keep the original ratio.

Talk about it

Would adding the same number to both parts preserve the recipe?

A little hint
  • Twenty scoops is four batches of five.
Answer & what to notice

Use 8 blue scoops and 12 yellow scoops; both original amounts are multiplied by 4.

Equal multiplication preserves the ratio; adding the same amount generally does not.

Make it easier

Scale the recipe to 10 scoops.

Take it further

Find the amounts for 7.5 scoops using fractional batches.

Away from the screen

Invent a bead-pattern ratio on paper and scale it to two different totals.

What you’ll need

  • Paper
  • Two coloured pencils or labelled symbols

Use what you have

Use B and Y labels instead of colours.

Grown-up note

This uses drawn scoops only; no paint mixing or tasting is needed.

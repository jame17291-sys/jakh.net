Keep the ice cool

Test whether a cloth covering slows melting under the same room conditions.

30 minIndependent readerPlay together

What we’re practising

Compare insulation while acknowledging limits in a small experiment.

Before you begin

Place bowls side by side on a tray away from sun, heaters and drafts.

Let’s try it

  1. Put one similar-sized ice cube in each bowl.
  2. Wrap one bowl loosely in the dry cloth and leave the other uncovered.
  3. Predict the result, then wait twenty minutes while drawing the setup.
  4. Uncover once and compare the remaining ice and meltwater.

Talk about it

If both cubes remain partly frozen, what can you still compare?

A little hint
  • Compare how much solid ice remains, not just whether any ice remains.
Answer & what to notice

The covered cube may melt more slowly; results depend on cloth, contact and room conditions, so report the observation honestly.

A cloth can slow heat transfer from warmer surroundings; it does not create cold or stop melting forever.

Make it easier

Observe the two setups with an adult describing the difference.

Take it further

Repeat with fresh equal cubes and a different dry wrapping material.

Away from the screen

Explain why an insulated lunch bag still needs an appropriate cooling source.

What you’ll need

  • Two similar ice cubes
  • Two identical shallow bowls
  • Dry folded cloth
  • Tray

Use what you have

If ice is unavailable, draw the setup and identify what would need to stay constant.

Grown-up note

An adult handles ice if uncomfortable to touch; keep meltwater contained and never place ice on skin for a prolonged period.

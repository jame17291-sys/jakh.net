A surface that slows a slide

Compare a block sliding down a smooth ramp and a fabric-covered ramp.

20 minIndependent readerPlay together

What we’re practising

Investigate how a surface can affect sliding while controlling slope.

Before you begin

Make a low ramp on a tray using one book; mark a release line near the top.

Let’s try it

  1. Place the block at the line and release without pushing.
  2. Record whether it slides freely, slowly or not at all.
  3. Cover the same ramp with cloth and repeat from the same line.
  4. Repeat each setup three times without changing ramp height.

Talk about it

Why would giving one trial a push make the comparison less fair?

A little hint
  • Start each trial in the same place and let go rather than shove.
Answer & what to notice

The cloth often slows or stops the block, but the exact result depends on both materials and the slope.

Friction opposes sliding; changing the contacting surface can change how strongly it resists motion.

Make it easier

Compare only one slide on each surface.

Take it further

Keep the surface fixed and separately investigate the effect of ramp height.

Away from the screen

Name situations where more friction helps, such as a grippy shoe sole.

What you’ll need

  • Stiff cardboard
  • One book
  • Small block or eraser
  • Dry cloth
  • Tray
  • Paper
  • Pencil

Use what you have

Use a flat safe board instead of cardboard.

Grown-up note

An adult stabilizes the low ramp; use light objects, contain them on a tray and never test on stairs or with people.

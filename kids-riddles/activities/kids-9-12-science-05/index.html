Kids Learning & Play / A model of day and night

Ages 9–12 · Science & nature

A model of day and night

Rotate a labelled ball in a steady beam of light to model a day.

20 minIndependent readerPlay together

What we’re practising

Use a model to explain changing illumination and identify its limitations.

Before you begin

An adult places a cool torch at one fixed position; add a marker to the ball for an imaginary town.

Let’s try it

  1. Hold the ball where one side is lit and the other is shaded.
  2. Slowly rotate it in place while watching the town marker.
  3. Describe when the town enters light, stays lit and moves into darkness.
  4. State one thing the model shows well and one thing it does not represent accurately.

Talk about it

Do you need to move the light around the ball to create this cycle?

A little hint
  • Keep the light fixed while the ball rotates around its own axis.
Answer & what to notice

The marker experiences light and darkness as the ball turns; Earth's rotation produces the day-night cycle.

The model represents rotation and illumination, but not the real sizes, distances, sunlight rays or exact day lengths.

Make it easier

Compare only the lit and unlit sides without rotating.

Take it further

Mark a second town on the opposite side and compare their illumination at the same time.

Away from the screen

Draw four positions of the same town during one full turn.

What you’ll need

  • Ball
  • Removable paper marker
  • Torch

Use what you have

Use a round fruit only as a model, keeping it clean and uneaten during play.

Grown-up note

Never look into the torch or the Sun; keep lights cool and paths clear.

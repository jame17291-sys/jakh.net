A little curiosity, a world of discovery

Give every child a way to join

Change the materials or response, while keeping the idea.

Offer another way to answer

A child can point, choose between two objects, speak, draw or ask you to write their words. Read one instruction at a time and demonstrate the first step. These options do not require a diagnosis or a special label.

Make the setup comfortable

Use larger pieces, a non-slip surface or high-contrast paper when useful. Keep objects within comfortable reach. Offer a seated version of a movement game and a quiet version of a sound game. Ask your child which setup they prefer.

Keep the learning purpose in view

If the purpose is sorting, moving the pieces for your child while they choose the groups still lets them practise sorting. If cutting is difficult, offer pre-cut shapes. Change the physical task without taking away the child’s decisions, and follow their cues about comfort.

Try one small thing today

Choose one idea from this guide and one activity your child is curious about. Adapt it together, and keep what works for your family.

Help without giving the answer

Small prompts that leave the discovery with your child.

Give thinking time

Read the question once, then pause. Let your child talk, point, draw or move objects. Silence can be thinking time. If they ask for help, begin with “What have you noticed?” rather than naming the answer.

Use a hint ladder

First repeat the goal in simpler words. Next draw attention to one useful clue. Then try a smaller example together. For a pattern such as red, blue, red, blue, point to the first pair and ask which pair repeats. Open the activity hint only when it is useful.

Explain after the reveal

Read the explanation together. Ask which clue fitted the answer and whether another answer could fit. If your child’s answer is reasonable, explore it rather than insisting on one word. Invite them to make a new clue for you.

Try one small thing today

Choose one idea from this guide and one activity your child is curious about. Adapt it together, and keep what works for your family.

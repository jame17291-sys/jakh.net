Kids Learning & Play / Turn a mistake into a question

A little curiosity, a world of discovery

Turn a mistake into a question

Explore what happened without scores or pressure.

Describe, do not label

Say what you observed: “That bridge bent when we added the third block.” Avoid labels such as clever, bad at maths or careless. A specific observation makes it easier to decide what to try next.

Change one thing

Ask your child to predict what might help, then change one thing and try again. For a paper bridge, fold the paper before adding the same blocks. Compare the attempts. If a puzzle answer does not fit, return to the clue that rules it out.

Let stopping be an option

If the activity becomes frustrating, offer a smaller step, a different way to respond, or a break. You can model “I am not sure yet; I will try another way.” Return later if your child wants to. A finished worksheet is not the only useful outcome.

Try one small thing today

Choose one idea from this guide and one activity your child is curious about. Adapt it together, and keep what works for your family.

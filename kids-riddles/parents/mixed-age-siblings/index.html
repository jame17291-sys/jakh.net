Play together across ages

One shared activity, with different ways to contribute.

Choose a shared task

Try a story, a building challenge or a sorting game with an easy entry point. A younger child can choose a character or group objects; an older child can explain a rule or record an observation. Let each child choose a role, and avoid making the older child responsible for teaching.

Vary the challenge, not the value

Use the easier and harder variations at the same table. One child might build a repeating two-colour pattern while another writes a rule for a longer pattern. Notice each contribution without comparing speed or correctness between siblings.

Make turns visible

Agree who chooses first and swap after a short round. Use a large turn card or simply say whose turn comes next. If cooperation is difficult today, let children make separate versions and share one thing they enjoyed at the end.

Try one small thing today

Choose one idea from this guide and one activity your child is curious about. Adapt it together, and keep what works for your family.

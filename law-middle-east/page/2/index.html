21What is ta'zir in Islamic criminal law?

Answer

Discretionary punishments imposed by a judge for offenses not covered by hudud or qisas, where the punishment type and severity is left to judicial discretion.

22What is riba in Islamic law and how is it defined?

Answer

Riba (ربا) means any increase or excess charged over a loan principal — broadly interpreted as interest. It is prohibited in Islam. Islamic finance uses profit-sharing instruments (mudarabah, musharakah) to achieve similar economic outcomes without charging fixed interest.

23What is the difference between talaq and khul' in Islamic divorce law?

Answer

Talaq is divorce initiated by the husband by pronouncing the divorce formula. Khul' is divorce initiated by the wife, who typically returns the mahr (dowry) to the husband in exchange for release from the marriage.

24What is a sukuk in Islamic finance?

Answer

Sukuk are Islamic financial certificates representing ownership stakes in assets or projects — the Islamic equivalent of bonds. They generate returns through profit-sharing or rent from the underlying asset rather than interest payments.

25What is the kafala (sponsorship) system common in Gulf states and how does it affect migrant workers?

Answer

The kafala system ties a migrant worker's legal residency and employment to a specific local sponsor (kafeel). Workers cannot change employers or leave the country without sponsor permission, creating dependency and vulnerability to exploitation. Several Gulf states have begun reforming or dismantling the system.

26What is the principle of non-intervention in international law?

Answer

The principle that states are forbidden from intervening in the internal affairs of other sovereign states — enshrined in Article 2(1) of the UN Charter. It prohibits coercive interference in political, economic, or social matters.

27What is the Cairo Declaration on Human Rights in Islam (1990)?

Answer

Adopted by the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), it affirms human rights but subordinates them to Islamic Sharia, offering an alternative framework to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. It is criticized for limiting rights to what Sharia permits.

28What is the burden of proof for hudud crimes in classical Islamic jurisprudence?

Answer

Extremely high — for example, zina (adultery/fornication) requires four male witnesses to the act itself. This effectively made enforcement rare in classical fiqh, with scholars arguing the strict standard was intended to limit actual punishment.

29What is murabaha in Islamic finance and how is it used for home or car purchases?

Answer

Murabaha is a cost-plus financing arrangement: the bank buys an asset outright and resells it to the customer at a disclosed markup, payable in installments. This avoids interest while achieving similar financing outcomes.

30What is the difference between a presidential and a parliamentary system of government?

Answer

In a presidential system (e.g. USA, Egypt), the executive is separate from the legislature and directly elected. In a parliamentary system (e.g. UK, Jordan), the executive (prime minister) is drawn from and accountable to the legislature.

31What is the principle of jus cogens in international law?

Answer

Peremptory norms of international law from which no derogation is permitted — such as the prohibitions on genocide, slavery, and torture. They cannot be overridden by treaty.

32What is the Islamic concept of wilaya in family law?

Answer

Wilaya is guardianship or protective authority — most commonly the male guardian's (wali) role in a woman's marriage, which classical fiqh requires for the marriage contract's validity. Contemporary Muslim-majority states vary widely in how strictly they apply this requirement.

33What is ijara in Islamic finance?

Answer

Ijara is an Islamic leasing contract where a bank or financier buys an asset and leases it to the customer for an agreed rental, with ownership optionally transferring at the end. It is used for vehicle and equipment financing as an interest-free alternative.

34What is the diya (blood money) system in Islamic law?

Answer

Diya is financial compensation paid to the victim or victim's family in cases of unintentional killing or bodily harm, as an alternative to qisas retaliation. Amounts vary by injury type and are still applied in several Muslim-majority legal systems.

35What is the International Court of Justice (ICJ) and what types of cases does it hear?

Answer

The principal judicial organ of the UN, based in The Hague. It settles legal disputes between states (contentious cases) and gives advisory opinions on legal questions referred by UN organs. Only states — not individuals — can be parties to contentious cases.

36What is the Arab Charter on Human Rights?

Answer

A human rights instrument adopted by the Arab League in 2004, entering into force in 2008. It guarantees civil, political, economic, social, and cultural rights, but is criticized for allowing derogation on national security grounds and for not fully aligning with international standards.

37What is the role of the Constitutional Court in Egypt's legal system?

Answer

Egypt's Supreme Constitutional Court has exclusive jurisdiction over the constitutionality of legislation, interprets legislative texts referred to it, and settles jurisdictional conflicts between courts. Its rulings are final and binding on all state organs.

38What is the Wage Protection System (WPS) used in UAE and Saudi Arabia?

Answer

An electronic salary monitoring system that requires employers to pay workers' wages through approved financial channels on time. Compliance is tracked by authorities, and non-paying employers face fines, work permit bans, or criminal prosecution.

39What is ijtihad in Islamic law and why was it historically controversial?

Answer

Ijtihad is independent legal reasoning to derive new rulings on matters not explicitly addressed in the Quran or Sunnah. It became controversial after classical scholars declared 'the closing of the gate of ijtihad' — suggesting most major legal questions had been settled — though this view is contested and modern reformists strongly advocate reopening ijtihad.

40What is arbitration and why is it commonly used in international commercial contracts in the Middle East?

Answer

Arbitration is a private dispute resolution process where parties agree to submit disputes to one or more arbitrators whose decision (award) is binding. In international trade, it is preferred over national courts because it is neutral, confidential, and awards are enforceable internationally under the New York Convention (1958).

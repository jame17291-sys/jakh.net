41What is the difference between a crime and a tort in most Arab civil law systems?

Answer

A crime is a wrong against society prosecuted by the state, resulting in criminal penalties (imprisonment, fines). A tort (civil wrong) is a wrong against an individual that gives rise to a private civil action for compensation. The same act can constitute both a crime and a tort simultaneously.

42What is the difference between ownership and possession in Arab civil law?

Answer

Ownership (milkiyya) is the full legal right to use, enjoy, and dispose of property. Possession (hiyaza) is the physical control of property, which may exist without ownership (e.g. a tenant possesses but doesn't own). Possession can give rise to certain legal protections even without full title.

43How do dual legal systems (mixed sharia-civil law) function in countries like Egypt, Jordan, and Kuwait?

Answer

These countries apply civil law codes (French-derived) for commercial and criminal matters while applying Sharia in personal status law (marriage, divorce, inheritance, guardianship). Separate courts (personal status or sharia courts) handle family matters, while civil and criminal courts handle the rest. Integration is complex — Sharia principles often inform constitutional interpretation even in the civil domain.

44What is the doctrine of maslaha (public interest) in Islamic jurisprudence and how has it been used in legal reform?

Answer

Maslaha holds that rulings may be derived or reformed to serve the public good, even if not explicitly derived from Quran or Sunnah texts, provided they do not contradict established principles. Reformist scholars use it to argue for updating Islamic law on issues like women's rights, banking, and criminal penalties to fit modern contexts.

45What is the Responsibility to Protect (R2P) doctrine and how has it been applied in the Arab world?

Answer

R2P holds that states have a primary responsibility to protect their populations from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing, and crimes against humanity. When a state fails, the international community may intervene. It was controversially invoked for the 2011 NATO intervention in Libya (UNSC Resolution 1973), raising debates about its limits and potential for misuse.

46What is the concept of shura in Islamic political theory and how do Gulf states incorporate it into governance?

Answer

Shura is the principle of consultation — rulers should consult with advisors or representatives before making decisions. Gulf states incorporate it through consultative councils (like Saudi Arabia's Majlis al-Shura or Qatar's Majlis) that advise the ruler but lack full legislative power, representing a partial institutionalization of shura principles.

47What is the role of a Sharia Supervisory Board in Islamic banking?

Answer

A Sharia Supervisory Board is a panel of qualified Islamic scholars who review and certify that a bank's financial products, contracts, and operations comply with Sharia principles. They issue fatwas on products and audit transactions. Their independence and consistency across institutions remains a subject of debate in Islamic finance governance.

48What are the Rome Statute and the International Criminal Court (ICC), and why have some Arab states not ratified them?

Answer

The Rome Statute (1998) established the ICC, the first permanent international criminal court with jurisdiction over genocide, crimes against humanity, and war crimes. Many Arab states have not ratified it due to concerns about sovereignty, potential politically motivated prosecutions, and the ICC's previous focus on African and Middle Eastern situations. Sudan's President al-Bashir faced ICC indictments for Darfur war crimes — highlighting tensions between accountability and sovereignty.

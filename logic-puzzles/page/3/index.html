41Two guards: one always lies, one always tells the truth. You may ask one guard one question to find the safe path. What do you ask?

Answer

Ask either guard: 'What would the other guard say is the safe path?' Then take the opposite. Both guards' answers point to the wrong path by this method.

42Two fathers and two sons go fishing. They each catch exactly one fish, yet only three fish are caught in total. How?

Answer

There are only three people: a grandfather, a father, and a son. The father counts as both a father and a son.

46A man lives on the 30th floor. Every morning he takes the elevator all the way down. On rainy evenings or when someone else is in the elevator, he rides to the 30th floor. Otherwise he stops at the 15th floor and walks up. Why?

Answer

He is short and can only reach the button for floor 15. On rainy days he uses his umbrella to press button 30, or asks someone else to press it.

47If every plank of a ship is gradually replaced, is it still the same ship? What if the original planks are then reassembled into a ship elsewhere?

Answer

This is the Ship of Theseus paradox. It challenges our concept of identity through change — with implications for personal identity: if all our cells are replaced over time, are we the same person?

48A runaway trolley will kill five people on the tracks. You can pull a lever to divert it to a side track where it will kill only one. Do you pull the lever?

Answer

This is the Trolley Problem in ethics. Pulling the lever saves five lives but makes you directly responsible for one death. It tests consequentialism (outcomes matter) against deontology (rules matter regardless of outcomes).

49A snail is at the bottom of a 10-metre well. Each day it climbs 3 metres and each night slips back 2 metres. How many days does it take to escape?

Answer

8 days. After 7 days the snail is at 7 m. On day 8 it climbs 3 m to reach 10 m and escapes before slipping back.

51You are in a room with three switches, each controlling one of three bulbs in another room. You can enter the other room only once. How do you determine which switch controls which bulb?

Answer

Turn on switch 1 for 10 minutes, then off. Turn on switch 2. Enter: the lit bulb is switch 2; the warm unlit bulb is switch 1; the cold unlit bulb is switch 3.

52100 prisoners are each in solitary cells. A warden picks prisoners one at a time at random to enter a room with a lamp that can be on or off; a prisoner may toggle it. At any point, a prisoner may declare that all 100 have visited. How can they guarantee success?

Answer

Appoint one 'counter' prisoner. Only the counter turns the lamp OFF (once per non-counter visit it was ON). When the counter has turned it off 99 times, all 100 have visited.

53What is the next number in this sequence: 1, 11, 21, 1211, 111221?

Answer

312211. It is the 'Look and Say' sequence — each term describes the previous: one 3, one 1, two 2s, two 1s.

54You have 12 identical-looking balls. One is either heavier or lighter than the rest. Using a balance scale exactly 3 times, find the odd ball and whether it is heavier or lighter.

Answer

Divide into three groups of 4. Weigh group A vs B: if balanced, the odd ball is in C and you use 2 more weighings to isolate it and determine its weight. If unbalanced, a systematic elimination across 2 more weighings reveals the odd ball.

55You have two ropes, each taking exactly 1 hour to burn, but with non-uniform burning rates. How do you measure exactly 45 minutes?

Answer

Light rope 1 at both ends and rope 2 at one end simultaneously. When rope 1 finishes (30 min), light rope 2's other end. When rope 2 finishes = 45 minutes total.

56On an island, every person is either a knight (always tells truth) or a knave (always lies). You meet two people, A and B. A says: 'At least one of us is a knave.' What are A and B?

Answer

A is a knight and B is a knave. If A were a knave, his statement ('at least one is a knave') would be true — a contradiction. So A is a knight telling the truth, which means B must be the knave.

57What is the last digit of 7¹⁰⁰?

Answer

1. The last digits of powers of 7 cycle in groups of 4: 7, 9, 3, 1. Since 100 ÷ 4 = 25 exactly, the last digit is 1.

58What is the formula for the number of diagonals in an n-sided polygon?

Answer

n(n−3)/2. Each vertex connects to n−3 others (excluding itself and its two adjacent vertices), divided by 2 to avoid double-counting.

59Gödel proved that in any consistent formal system powerful enough to express arithmetic, there are true statements that cannot be proved within that system. What is the name of this result and what does it imply?

Answer

Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem (1931). It implies mathematics cannot be both complete and consistent — no formal system can prove all truths about arithmetic, demolishing Hilbert's program to found all math on a complete, consistent axiomatic basis.

60Crack the three-digit code. Each clue is exact: 682—one digit is correct and well placed; 614—one digit is correct but wrongly placed; 206—two digits are correct but wrongly placed; 738—no digit is correct; 780—one digit is correct but wrongly placed. What is the code?

Answer

042. Clue 738 removes 7, 3, and 8. Clue 780 then places 0 in the code but not third. Clues 682 and 614 eliminate 6 and put 2 third. Clue 206 puts 0 first, leaving 4 in the middle.

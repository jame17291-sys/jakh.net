61What is the difference between benign and malignant tumours?

Answer

Benign tumours grow locally without invading or metastasising; malignant tumours invade surrounding tissue and can spread to distant sites

65What is disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)?

Answer

A condition where widespread clot formation throughout the body simultaneously depletes clotting factors, causing both clotting and bleeding

66Where do loop diuretics act in the nephron, and which transporter do they inhibit?

Answer

They act in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle and inhibit the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter (NKCC2).

67What is the Frank-Starling mechanism?

Answer

Cardiac output increases as venous return (preload) increases, because greater stretch of cardiac muscle produces stronger contraction

69Why does acetaminophen overdose injure the liver, and what antidote is used?

Answer

Excess production of the toxic metabolite NAPQI depletes glutathione and damages hepatocytes. N-acetylcysteine replenishes glutathione and is the antidote.

73What is the difference between Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis?

Answer

Crohn's: any part of the GI tract, transmural, skip lesions, granulomas; UC: limited to colon, mucosal only, continuous lesions, no granulomas

74What is the mechanism of action of opioids?

Answer

Bind to mu, delta, and kappa opioid receptors (GPCRs), inhibiting adenylyl cyclase and reducing neuronal excitability — producing analgesia, euphoria, and respiratory depression

75What is the oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve and what shifts it?

Answer

An S-shaped curve describing oxygen binding affinity; right shift (Bohr effect): low pH, high CO₂, high temperature, high 2,3-DPG — all promoting O₂ release to tissues

76What is pharmacokinetics vs pharmacodynamics?

Answer

Pharmacokinetics: what the body does to the drug (ADME — absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion); Pharmacodynamics: what the drug does to the body

77What is the Warburg effect?

Answer

Cancer cells preferentially use aerobic glycolysis (rather than oxidative phosphorylation) even in the presence of oxygen — generates rapid ATP and biosynthetic precursors

78What is the complement cascade and its three activation pathways?

Answer

Classical pathway (antibody-antigen), lectin pathway (mannose on pathogens), alternative pathway (spontaneous C3 hydrolysis) — all converge to form MAC (membrane attack complex)

79What is the concept of therapeutic index (TI)?

Answer

TI = TD50/ED50 — the ratio of toxic dose to effective dose; a narrow TI means a drug requires careful monitoring (e.g. digoxin, lithium, warfarin)

80What are the hallmarks of cancer (Hanahan & Weinberg)?

Answer

Sustained proliferative signalling, evading growth suppressors, resisting cell death, enabling replicative immortality, inducing angiogenesis, activating invasion and metastasis, and others

21What was the significance of the Battle of Marathon (490 BC) in the context of Persian expansion?

Answer

The Athenians repelled the Persian invasion force of Darius I at Marathon, halting Persian expansion into mainland Greece and becoming a symbol of Greek resistance to empire.

22What was the Battle of Karbala (680 CE) and why is it significant in Islamic history?

Answer

The battle in which Husayn ibn Ali, grandson of Prophet Muhammad, was killed by the Umayyad army. It is the pivotal event in the Shia-Sunni split and is commemorated annually by Shia Muslims during Ashura.

23Who was Ibn Khaldun and what is his major contribution to intellectual history?

Answer

Ibn Khaldun (1332–1406) was a North African Arab historian and philosopher who wrote the Muqaddimah — considered the first systematic study of history and sociology, analyzing cycles of civilization, group solidarity (asabiyyah), and the rise and fall of states.

24What was the Sykes-Picot Agreement (1916)?

Answer

A secret Anglo-French agreement that divided much of the Middle East into British and French spheres of influence following the anticipated collapse of the Ottoman Empire. Its borders roughly correspond to modern Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, and Jordan.

25What was the 1967 Six-Day War?

Answer

A war in which Israel defeated Egypt, Jordan, and Syria in six days, capturing the Sinai Peninsula, Gaza Strip, West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Golan Heights. It fundamentally reshaped the Israeli-Palestinian conflict and the broader regional balance of power.

Answer

27What was the Oslo Accords (1993) and what was its significance?

Answer

A peace framework between Israel and the PLO, signed in Washington, where the PLO recognized Israel and Israel recognized the PLO as the representative of Palestinians. It established the Palestinian Authority but left final status issues (Jerusalem, borders, refugees) unresolved.

28What was the Lebanon Civil War and how long did it last?

Answer

A multi-factional civil war in Lebanon from 1975 to 1990, involving Lebanese political factions, Palestinian groups, Israel, Syria, and later various militias. It killed an estimated 120,000–150,000 people.

30What was the Code of Hammurabi and why is it historically significant?

Answer

One of the earliest and most complete written legal codes, created by the Babylonian king Hammurabi around 1754 BC. It codified 282 laws covering trade, property, family, and criminal matters and established the principle that the state enforces law — not just the aggrieved party.

31What was the Battle of Tours (732 CE) and why is it considered pivotal in world history?

Answer

The battle where Frankish and Burgundian forces under Charles Martel defeated the Umayyad Muslim army, halting the northward advance of Islamic expansion into western Europe. Many historians consider it a turning point preventing the Islamization of Europe.

32What were the Tanzimat reforms of the 19th-century Ottoman Empire?

Answer

A series of modernizing reforms (1839–1876) that reorganized the military, bureaucracy, taxation, and courts; granted legal equality to non-Muslims; and introduced a secular penal code — representing the Ottoman Empire's attempt to modernize and compete with European powers.

33What was the mandate system established after World War I and how did it shape the modern Middle East?

Answer

The League of Nations mandates gave Britain and France administrative control over former Ottoman territories under the guise of 'preparing them for independence'. Britain took Iraq, Palestine, and Transjordan; France took Syria and Lebanon. These arbitrary borders created states that often lacked national cohesion and generated conflicts still felt today.

34What was Pan-Arabism and which leader was most associated with it?

Answer

Pan-Arabism was an ideology promoting political unity among Arab peoples based on shared language, culture, and history. Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt was its foremost champion. The United Arab Republic — the short-lived 1958 union of Egypt and Syria — was its most concrete expression before dissolving in 1961.

35What was the Crusades and how did they affect the Middle East?

Answer

A series of religious military campaigns (1095–1291) launched by European Christians to recover the Holy Land from Muslim rule. They established short-lived Crusader states in Palestine, Lebanon, and Syria. The conflict produced significant cultural exchange but also great violence — ultimately ending with Muslim reconquest. Their legacy shaped Muslim-Christian relations for centuries.

36What was the Iran-Iraq War (1980–1988) and what triggered it?

Answer

Triggered by Iraq's invasion of Iran under Saddam Hussein, partly motivated by Saddam's fear of Iran's Islamic Revolution inspiring Shia Iraqis, and territorial disputes over the Shatt al-Arab waterway. The war lasted 8 years, killed an estimated 1 million people, and ended in a stalemate with no border changes.

37What was the Camp David Accords (1978) and who were the key figures involved?

Answer

Framework agreements brokered by US President Jimmy Carter between Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin. They led to the 1979 Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty, returned Sinai to Egypt, but Sadat was assassinated in 1981 partly because of Arab world opposition to the deal.

38What was the significance of the discovery of oil in Saudi Arabia in 1938?

Answer

The discovery of vast oil reserves at Dammam Well No. 7 transformed Saudi Arabia from a poor country into the world's largest oil exporter, gave birth to the modern Gulf economy, created ARAMCO, and gave Saudi Arabia extraordinary geopolitical leverage — reshaping global energy markets and Middle East politics permanently.

39What was the significance of the 1973 Yom Kippur War (October War)?

Answer

Egypt and Syria launched a surprise attack against Israel on Yom Kippur to recover territories lost in 1967. Initial Arab gains were reversed but the war restored Arab military credibility, led to the Arab oil embargo (1973 oil crisis) that shocked Western economies, and ultimately paved the way for the Camp David peace process.

40What was the Nabataean Kingdom and what is its most famous city?

Answer

The Nabataeans were an Arab people who built a prosperous trading kingdom in the Negev desert and Jordan from around 300 BC to 106 AD, controlling incense and spice trade routes. Their most famous city is Petra, the rock-carved city in modern Jordan.

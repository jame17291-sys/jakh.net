41What was the Oslo II (Interim Agreement) of 1995 and what did it divide?

Answer

Oslo II divided the West Bank into three zones: Area A (full Palestinian civil and security control), Area B (Palestinian civil control, joint security), and Area C (full Israeli control, covering ~60% of West Bank). This division has remained in place, shaping access, settlement expansion, and daily Palestinian life.

42What were the Abraham Accords (2020)?

Answer

Normalization agreements brokered by the US Trump administration between Israel and the UAE, Bahrain, Sudan, and Morocco. They established diplomatic relations and trade without requiring resolution of the Palestinian issue — a departure from the traditional Arab position that normalization required a Palestinian state first.

43What was the Phoenician civilization known for?

Answer

The Phoenicians, based in the eastern Mediterranean (modern Lebanon), were master traders and seafarers who established colonies across the Mediterranean including Carthage. Most significantly, they developed an early alphabet that became the ancestor of most modern alphabets including Arabic, Greek, Latin, and Hebrew.

44What factors led to the founding of the PLO (Palestine Liberation Organization) in 1964?

Answer

The 1948 Nakba had displaced hundreds of thousands of Palestinians. Arab states sponsored the PLO's founding at the Arab League summit in Cairo to represent Palestinian national aspirations and organize resistance against Israel. Yasser Arafat's Fatah movement gained control in 1969.

45What was the Fatimid Caliphate and where was it centered?

Answer

A Shia Ismaili dynasty that ruled North Africa and Egypt (909–1171 CE), centered in Cairo, which the Fatimids founded as their capital. At its peak it controlled Egypt, the Levant, and parts of the Maghreb. The Al-Azhar mosque and university, founded by the Fatimids, remains one of the world's most influential Islamic institutions.

46What was the Mu'tazila movement in Islamic intellectual history and why did it decline?

Answer

The Mu'tazilites (8th–10th centuries) were rationalist theologians who argued the Quran was created (not eternal), that human reason can determine ethical truth, and that God's justice requires free human will. They enjoyed Abbasid patronage during the Mihna (833–848 CE) — a state-enforced inquisition requiring scholars to affirm Quranic createdness. The backlash led to the Ash'ari school's dominance, which emphasized revelation over reason, and the Mu'tazilites were largely suppressed.

47How did the Ottoman millet system function and why was it significant for the governance of diverse populations?

Answer

The millet system recognized non-Muslim religious communities (Greeks, Armenians, Jews) as semi-autonomous corporate bodies governed by their own religious laws and leaders in personal status matters (marriage, inheritance, religious education) while remaining Ottoman subjects. It enabled a religiously diverse empire to function with minimal state intervention in minority affairs — a form of legal pluralism with both protective and segregating effects.

48How did the 1973 oil embargo reshape the global economic and political order?

Answer

OPEC's Arab members cut oil production and embargoed the US and Netherlands for supporting Israel in the Yom Kippur War. Oil prices quadrupled, triggering global recession, stagflation, and a shift in Western energy policy toward conservation and diversification. It demonstrated the geopolitical leverage of commodity control, accelerated petrodollar recycling through Western banks, and permanently elevated Gulf Arab states in global finance.

49What was the Mongol sack of Baghdad (1258) and what was its impact on Islamic civilization?

Answer

Hulagu Khan's Mongol forces killed the Abbasid Caliph, reportedly massacred 200,000–800,000 inhabitants, and destroyed the city including the House of Wisdom. It ended the Abbasid Caliphate, broke the centralized political-religious authority of the Islamic world, and is considered one of the most devastating events in Islamic history — though Egypt under the Mamluks defeated the Mongols at Ain Jalut shortly after.

50What are the underlying structural factors that historians identify as causes of the Arab Spring's failure to consolidate democracy?

Answer

Historians point to: weak civil society institutions (decades of authoritarian suppression); deep sectarian, tribal, and regional divisions exploited by old-guard actors; militaries unwilling to cede power (Egypt's coup, Syria's Assad); economic deterioration and lack of inclusive development; international interference (Gulf states and Western powers backing preferred factions); and the absence of a middle-class large enough to sustain democratic transitions as occurred in some East European post-communist states.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What class of drugs reduces fever and relieves pain, with paracetamol (acetaminophen) as a common example?

Analgesics/antipyretics (pain relievers and fever reducers).

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What class of drugs kills or inhibits bacteria?

Antibiotics (antibacterials).

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What class of drugs blocks histamine receptors and is used to treat allergies?

Antihistamines (H1-receptor antagonists).

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What is a diuretic and what condition is it commonly used to treat?

A drug that increases urine production, commonly used to treat hypertension (high blood pressure) and edema (fluid retention).

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What class of drugs lowers blood glucose levels and is used to treat diabetes?

Antidiabetic drugs — including insulin (injected) and oral hypoglycemics like metformin.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Pharmacokinetics & Biopharmaceutics

What does the term 'half-life' of a drug mean?

The time it takes for the concentration of a drug in the body to decrease by half. It determines how frequently a drug must be dosed.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Safety & Interactions

What is the difference between a side effect and an adverse drug reaction?

A side effect is any secondary, usually unintended effect of a drug at therapeutic doses. An adverse drug reaction (ADR) is a harmful, unintended response at normal doses — a subset of side effects that causes harm.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What is the mechanism of action of NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) like ibuprofen?

NSAIDs inhibit the enzyme cyclooxygenase (COX), which reduces prostaglandin synthesis — thereby decreasing inflammation, pain, and fever.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Pharmacokinetics & Biopharmaceutics

What does 'oral bioavailability' of a drug mean?

The fraction of an orally administered drug that reaches the systemic circulation unchanged. A drug with 100% bioavailability is fully absorbed; low bioavailability means much is destroyed before reaching the bloodstream.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What are statins and what condition do they treat?

Statins (e.g. atorvastatin, simvastatin) are drugs that inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, reducing cholesterol synthesis in the liver. They treat hypercholesterolemia (high cholesterol) and reduce cardiovascular risk.

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What are beta-blockers and what conditions are they used for?

Drugs that block beta-adrenergic receptors, slowing the heart rate and reducing blood pressure. Used for hypertension, angina, heart failure, and arrhythmias (e.g. atenolol, metoprolol).

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Safety & Interactions

What is a contraindication in pharmacology?

A specific situation or condition in which a drug should NOT be used because it may be harmful to the patient — for example, aspirin is contraindicated in children with viral infections (Reye's syndrome risk).

💊 Pharmacy Easy Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What is the difference between a generic drug and a branded drug?

A branded drug is the original patented formulation. A generic drug contains the same active ingredient, same dose, same route, and meets bioequivalence standards — but is sold under its chemical name at lower cost once the patent expires.

💊 Pharmacy Medium Pharmacokinetics & Biopharmaceutics

What are the four pharmacokinetic processes described by ADME?

Absorption (drug enters circulation), Distribution (drug spreads to tissues), Metabolism (drug is chemically altered, mainly by liver), and Excretion (drug is eliminated, mainly by kidneys).

💊 Pharmacy Medium Drug Safety & Interactions

What is a drug-drug interaction and give one clinically important example?

When one drug affects the activity of another. Example: Warfarin (blood thinner) combined with aspirin increases bleeding risk because both inhibit clotting through different mechanisms — dose adjustment and monitoring are required.

💊 Pharmacy Medium Antimicrobials & Resistance

What is antibiotic resistance and how does it develop?

The ability of bacteria to survive and grow despite antibiotic treatment. It develops when bacteria acquire mutations or resistance genes — accelerated by overuse, incomplete courses, and use in livestock. Resistant bacteria spread, making infections harder to treat.

💊 Pharmacy Medium Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What are ACE inhibitors and what do they treat?

Drugs that block the angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), preventing conversion of angiotensin I to the vasoconstrictor angiotensin II. They lower blood pressure and are first-line for hypertension and heart failure (e.g. lisinopril, enalapril).

💊 Pharmacy Medium Pharmacodynamics & Receptors

What is the difference between an agonist and an antagonist in pharmacology?

An agonist binds to a receptor and activates it, producing a biological response. An antagonist binds to the receptor but does NOT activate it — it blocks agonists from binding, reducing a response.

💊 Pharmacy Medium Drug Safety & Interactions

What is the therapeutic index of a drug?

The ratio between the toxic dose and the effective dose (TD50/ED50). A wide therapeutic index means a drug is relatively safe; a narrow therapeutic index (e.g. warfarin, lithium, digoxin) means small dose changes can cause toxicity or loss of effect.

💊 Pharmacy Medium Drug Classes & Therapeutics

What is the mechanism of action of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like omeprazole?

PPIs irreversibly block the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump) in gastric parietal cells, stopping acid secretion. They treat peptic ulcers, GERD, and H. pylori infections.

41What are the main molecular mechanisms by which bacteria develop antibiotic resistance?

Answer

1. Enzymatic inactivation (e.g. beta-lactamases destroying penicillins). 2. Target modification (altered penicillin-binding proteins in MRSA). 3. Efflux pumps (actively expelling antibiotic from cell). 4. Reduced permeability (decreased outer membrane porins). 5. Target bypass (alternative metabolic pathways). Resistance genes can transfer between bacteria via plasmids (horizontal gene transfer).

42What is the difference between a competitive reversible antagonist and a non-competitive irreversible antagonist?

Answer

A competitive reversible antagonist competes with the agonist for the same binding site — its effect can be overcome by increasing agonist concentration (the dose-response curve shifts right but maximum effect is maintained). A non-competitive/irreversible antagonist binds covalently or at an allosteric site, and its effect cannot be overcome by more agonist — maximum effect is reduced even at high agonist doses.

43What is 'prodrug design' and give a clinically important example?

Answer

A prodrug is an inactive compound that is converted in the body to its active form. Prodrug design improves bioavailability, stability, or reduces toxicity. Example: Enalapril is an ester prodrug of enalaprilat — the active ACE inhibitor — because enalaprilat is poorly absorbed orally but enalapril's ester is absorbed well and hydrolyzed to enalaprilat after absorption.

44What are monoclonal antibodies and how are they used as drugs?

Answer

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) are laboratory-produced antibodies that bind a specific target (antigen) with high precision. As drugs, they can block disease-causing proteins (e.g. trastuzumab blocks HER2 in breast cancer; adalimumab blocks TNF-α in rheumatoid arthritis; rituximab depletes CD20-positive B cells in lymphoma). They often have suffix -mab in their name.

45What is the concept of 'mechanism-based inhibition' (suicide inhibition) of CYP450 enzymes?

Answer

Some drugs are metabolized by CYP450 enzymes into reactive intermediates that permanently inactivate the enzyme — 'committing suicide.' This causes irreversible drug interactions lasting until new enzyme is synthesized (days–weeks). Examples: erythromycin (CYP3A4), isoniazid (CYP2E1), grapefruit furanocoumarins (CYP3A4 in gut wall). Drug levels of CYP3A4 substrates can rise dramatically when grapefruit is consumed.

46What is 'biopharmaceutics classification system' (BCS) and how does it guide drug formulation?

Answer

The BCS classifies drugs by aqueous solubility and intestinal permeability into 4 classes: I (high solubility, high permeability — ideal), II (low solubility, high permeability — formulation key to improving dissolution), III (high solubility, low permeability — absorption enhancement needed), IV (low solubility, low permeability — most challenging). BCS guides whether modified-release or special delivery systems are needed for adequate bioavailability.

47What is the mechanism of action of checkpoint inhibitors in cancer immunotherapy?

Answer

Tumor cells evade immune destruction by expressing inhibitory checkpoint proteins (PD-L1, CTLA-4) that suppress T-cell activity. Checkpoint inhibitors (e.g. nivolumab, pembrolizumab block PD-1; ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4) remove these brakes, allowing T cells to recognize and kill cancer cells. This can cause immune-related adverse effects in normal tissues.

48What are lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) and why were they critical to mRNA vaccine delivery?

Answer

Lipid nanoparticles are tiny spherical vesicles made of lipids that encapsulate and protect mRNA from degradation and facilitate its delivery into cells. They fuse with cell membranes, releasing mRNA into the cytoplasm. This technology, developed over decades, was essential for COVID-19 mRNA vaccines (Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna) — solving the major challenge that naked mRNA is rapidly degraded in the body.

49What is the difference between pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic drug tolerance?

Answer

Pharmacokinetic tolerance: the body metabolizes or excretes the drug faster over time (enzyme induction), reducing plasma levels after the same dose. Pharmacodynamic tolerance: the drug concentration is unchanged but the target response is reduced — via receptor downregulation, desensitization, or compensatory physiological changes. Both mechanisms can contribute simultaneously.

50What is PBPK (physiologically based pharmacokinetic) modelling and what is its value in drug development?

Answer

PBPK models use mathematical equations representing actual organ volumes, blood flows, and biochemical processes to simulate drug distribution and elimination throughout the whole body. They can predict drug behavior in special populations (children, pregnant women, patients with renal/hepatic impairment) without exposing them to the drug — reducing costly animal studies and informing regulatory submissions for dose adjustment recommendations.

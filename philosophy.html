🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

Who is considered the father of Western philosophy?

Socrates (470–399 BC), the Athenian philosopher who developed the Socratic method of questioning. He wrote nothing himself — his ideas are known through his student Plato.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

What is Plato's theory of Forms?

Plato argued that the physical world is an imperfect copy of a higher realm of perfect, eternal abstract entities called Forms or Ideas. For example, all beautiful things participate in the Form of Beauty itself, which exists independently of any physical example.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

What is Aristotle's concept of the 'Golden Mean'?

The ethical principle that virtue lies between two extremes (excess and deficiency). For example, courage is the mean between cowardice (deficiency) and recklessness (excess). This is part of his virtue ethics framework.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

What is René Descartes' most famous philosophical statement?

'Cogito, ergo sum' — 'I think, therefore I am.' It was his foundational certainty: even if everything else could be doubted, the very act of doubting proves the existence of a thinking subject.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Ethics

What is utilitarianism?

The ethical theory that the right action is the one that maximizes overall happiness or utility for the greatest number of people. Associated with Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Ethics

What is Immanuel Kant's categorical imperative?

Kant's supreme principle of morality: 'Act only according to that maxim by which you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law.' In other words, only do what you could will everyone to do.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Epistemology & Logic

What is epistemology?

The branch of philosophy that studies the nature, sources, scope, and limits of knowledge. Key questions: What is knowledge? How do we know anything? What is the difference between belief and justified belief?

🧠 Philosophy Easy Metaphysics & Mind

What is the mind-body problem in philosophy?

The question of how the immaterial mind (thoughts, consciousness) relates to the physical brain and body. Descartes' dualism says mind and body are separate substances. Physicalism says mind is entirely a product of physical brain processes.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

What did Plato's allegory of the cave illustrate?

Prisoners in a cave see only shadows on a wall and think this is reality. When one escapes and sees the real world and sun, he returns to tell the others who refuse to believe him. It illustrates how most people mistake appearances for reality, and how true knowledge requires philosophical 'enlightenment'.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Political Philosophy

What is the social contract theory associated with Locke, Hobbes, and Rousseau?

The theory that legitimate political authority derives from an agreement (explicit or implicit) among individuals to form a society and give up certain freedoms to a government in exchange for security and social order.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

What is Stoicism?

A school of ancient Greek and Roman philosophy (founded by Zeno of Citium) teaching that virtue alone is sufficient for happiness, emotions should be governed by reason, and we should accept with equanimity what we cannot control.

🧠 Philosophy Easy Epistemology & Logic

What is a logical fallacy?

An error in reasoning that makes an argument invalid or misleading. Examples: ad hominem (attacking the person rather than the argument), straw man (misrepresenting an opponent's argument), false dichotomy (presenting only two options when more exist).

🧠 Philosophy Easy Ethics

What is the trolley problem in ethics?

A thought experiment: a runaway trolley will kill five people on the tracks. You can divert it to a side track where it will kill only one. Do you act? It tests whether the ethics of an action depends on outcomes (consequentialism) or on rules regardless of outcomes (deontology).

🧠 Philosophy Easy Philosophical Traditions

Who was Ibn Rushd (Averroes) and why was he significant in the history of philosophy?

Ibn Rushd (1126–1198) was an Andalusian Muslim philosopher who wrote extensive commentaries on Aristotle. His work transmitted Aristotle's philosophy to medieval Europe, influencing Aquinas and scholasticism. He argued for the compatibility of reason and faith, believing philosophy and theology could be reconciled.

🧠 Philosophy Medium Epistemology & Logic

What is the Gettier problem in epistemology?

Edmund Gettier (1963) challenged the classical definition of knowledge as 'justified true belief' with counterexamples where someone has a justified true belief that is true by accident — suggesting they don't really 'know' it. This sparked decades of debate about what conditions are sufficient for knowledge.

🧠 Philosophy Medium Ethics

What is the difference between deontological and consequentialist ethics?

Deontological ethics (Kant) judges actions as right or wrong based on whether they follow moral rules or duties — regardless of consequences. Consequentialist ethics (utilitarianism) judges actions by their outcomes — an action is right if it produces good consequences.

🧠 Philosophy Medium Political Philosophy

What is John Rawls' 'veil of ignorance' thought experiment?

Rawls asked: what principles of justice would rational people choose if they didn't know their own position in society (their wealth, class, race, talents)? Behind this 'veil of ignorance,' he argued, people would choose equal basic liberties and arrange inequalities to benefit the least advantaged — his 'difference principle.'

🧠 Philosophy Medium Metaphysics & Mind

What is Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles?

The principle that if two things are indistinguishable in all their properties, they are in fact the same thing. In other words, there cannot be two numerically distinct objects that are qualitatively identical in every respect.

🧠 Philosophy Medium Philosophical Traditions

What was the central debate between Al-Ghazali and Ibn Rushd?

Al-Ghazali wrote 'The Incoherence of the Philosophers' (Tahafut al-Falasifa) attacking Greek-influenced Islamic philosophy as heretical on issues like the eternity of the world and divine knowledge. Ibn Rushd responded with 'The Incoherence of the Incoherence' defending philosophy and rational inquiry.

🧠 Philosophy Medium Language & Philosophy of Science

What is Karl Popper's criterion of falsifiability?

Popper argued that a scientific theory must be falsifiable — it must make predictions that could, in principle, be proven wrong by observation or experiment. Theories that can explain any outcome (unfalsifiable) are not scientific. This demarcated science from pseudoscience.

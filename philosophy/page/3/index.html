41What is epistemic injustice and what are its two main forms as described by Miranda Fricker?

Answer

Epistemic injustice is a wrong done to someone specifically in their capacity as a knower. Miranda Fricker identifies: (1) Testimonial injustice — giving a speaker deflated credibility due to identity prejudice (e.g. disbelieving someone because of their gender or race); (2) Hermeneutical injustice — having inadequate conceptual resources to understand one's own experience (e.g. having no concept for 'sexual harassment' before it was named).

42What is the distinction between de re and de dicto modality in modal logic?

Answer

De dicto modality applies to propositions: 'Necessarily, the number of planets is greater than 7' means the proposition is necessarily true. De re modality applies to objects: 'The number of planets necessarily has this property' makes a claim about the object itself. The distinction matters because: 'Necessarily, the Morning Star is the Evening Star' (de dicto) is false (it's a contingent astronomical fact), while 'Hesperus is necessarily Hesperus' (de re) is true.

43What is the Duhem-Quine thesis in philosophy of science?

Answer

The thesis that scientific hypotheses cannot be tested in isolation — any test involves auxiliary hypotheses (background assumptions, instrument calibration, ceteris paribus conditions). When a prediction fails, we cannot know if the main hypothesis is wrong or one of the auxiliaries is at fault. This means no single experiment can definitively refute a theory — only a whole web of beliefs can be tested as a unit.

44What is the difference between negative and positive liberty as described by Isaiah Berlin?

Answer

Negative liberty: freedom from external constraints and interference (the absence of obstacles). Positive liberty: freedom to actually pursue one's goals and realize one's potential — which may require resources and conditions. Berlin warned that positive liberty can be co-opted by authoritarian regimes to justify imposing a 'true' freedom on citizens.

45What is the repugnant conclusion in population ethics (Derek Parfit)?

Answer

Parfit showed that totalist utilitarianism (maximize total welfare) implies that a world with billions of barely-worth-living lives would be better than a much smaller world of very happy people — as long as the sum total of welfare is higher. This 'repugnant conclusion' seems deeply wrong but is hard to avoid without abandoning standard utilitarian principles. It generates much of the literature in population ethics.

46What is Kripke's causal theory of reference and how does it challenge Frege-Russell descriptivism?

Answer

Frege-Russell descriptivism held that proper names refer by being associated with descriptions (Aristotle = 'the teacher of Alexander'). Kripke argued names are 'rigid designators' — they refer to the same object in all possible worlds, regardless of which descriptions are true. Reference is fixed causally: an initial baptism creates a chain of communication. Names still refer even when all believed descriptions are false.

47What is the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism?

Answer

Rawls and Nozick argued that utilitarianism fails to take seriously the fact that people are distinct individuals. Maximizing aggregate welfare can justify sacrificing one person for the benefit of many — treating people as mere vessels of utility rather than ends in themselves. This fails to respect the separateness of persons — you cannot compensate my suffering with someone else's happiness, because they are not the same person.

48What is the debate between presentism and eternalism in the philosophy of time?

Answer

Presentism holds that only the present moment exists — the past is gone and the future does not yet exist. Eternalism (block universe theory) holds that all times — past, present, and future — are equally real and co-exist in a four-dimensional spacetime block. Eternalism is compatible with special relativity (which shows simultaneity is relative), while presentism struggles to accommodate relativity theory.

49What is structural realism in philosophy of science and how does it respond to the pessimistic meta-induction?

Answer

The pessimistic meta-induction (Laudan) notes that past successful theories (Newtonian mechanics, phlogiston, ether) were later shown false — so current theories are probably false too. Structural realism (Worrall) responds that even when theories are replaced, their mathematical structure is preserved (Newton's equations are a limit case of Einstein's). Science successfully tracks structure even if it doesn't track underlying entities.

50What is the distinction between agent-relative and agent-neutral values in ethics?

Answer

Agent-neutral values are equally important to everyone regardless of who the agent is (e.g. reducing total suffering is equally important to all). Agent-relative values are indexed to particular agents — I have special reasons to promote my own projects, relationships, or commitments that others don't share equally. Consequentialism tends to endorse only agent-neutral values; deontology and virtue ethics accommodate agent-relative permissions and requirements (e.g. special duties to family).

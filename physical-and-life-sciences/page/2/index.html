21What causes a rain shadow on the leeward side of a mountain range?

Answer

Moist air rises and rains on the windward side; the descending air on the leeward side warms and becomes drier.

22What is the difference between ionic and covalent bonds?

Answer

Ionic bonds involve the transfer of electrons between atoms (usually metal + non-metal), while covalent bonds involve the sharing of electron pairs (usually non-metals).

23What is the role of ribosomes in the cell?

Answer

Ribosomes synthesize proteins by translating messenger RNA (mRNA) into amino acid chains.

24What is Ohm's Law?

Answer

V = IR: the voltage across a conductor equals the current through it multiplied by its resistance.

25What is Le Chatelier's principle?

Answer

If a system at equilibrium is disturbed (by changing concentration, pressure, or temperature), the equilibrium will shift to partially oppose the change.

26What is the function of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) in a eukaryotic cell?

Answer

The rough ER (studded with ribosomes) synthesizes and folds proteins; the smooth ER synthesizes lipids and detoxifies compounds.

27What does Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle state?

Answer

It is impossible to simultaneously know both the exact position and exact momentum of a particle with arbitrary precision; Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2.

28What is the difference between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion?

Answer

Fission splits a heavy nucleus into smaller ones (used in nuclear reactors); fusion merges light nuclei into heavier ones (powers the Sun). Both release enormous energy.

29What is the central dogma of molecular biology?

Answer

Genetic information flows from DNA → RNA → protein: DNA is transcribed into mRNA, which is translated into proteins.

30What is entropy in thermodynamics, and what does the second law state about it?

Answer

Entropy is a measure of disorder or the number of microstates in a system. The second law states that in an isolated system, entropy never decreases; all spontaneous processes increase total entropy.

31What is a catalyst and how does it speed up a reaction without being consumed?

Answer

A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. It participates in intermediates but is regenerated by the end of the reaction.

32What is allosteric regulation of an enzyme?

Answer

A regulator binds at a site other than the active site and changes the enzyme's activity by altering its shape.

33What is wave-particle duality?

Answer

Quantum objects (electrons, photons) exhibit both wave-like and particle-like behaviour depending on how they are observed. The double-slit experiment demonstrates this duality.

34What is the difference between a strong acid and a weak acid?

Answer

A strong acid fully dissociates in water (e.g., HCl, H₂SO₄). A weak acid only partially dissociates and exists in equilibrium with its conjugate base (e.g., acetic acid, Ka ≪ 1).

35How does the CRISPR-Cas9 system work?

Answer

A guide RNA (gRNA) directs the Cas9 protein to a specific DNA sequence, where Cas9 makes a double-strand cut. The cell's repair mechanisms can then delete, correct, or insert genes.

36What is the greenhouse effect and how does it cause climate change?

Answer

Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O) trap infrared radiation re-emitted by Earth's surface. Increased anthropogenic emissions enhance this effect, raising global average temperatures and disrupting climate systems.

37What is the photoelectric effect, and why was it significant for quantum theory?

Answer

When light strikes a metal surface, electrons are ejected — but only if the light's frequency exceeds a threshold. Einstein showed this required photons (quantised packets of energy), contradicting classical wave theory and earning him the 1921 Nobel Prize.

38What is epigenetics?

Answer

The study of heritable changes in gene expression that do not involve changes to the DNA sequence itself — caused by mechanisms such as DNA methylation and histone modification.

39What is quantum entanglement and why did Einstein call it 'spooky action at a distance'?

Answer

Two entangled particles share a quantum state such that measuring one instantly determines the state of the other, regardless of distance. Einstein objected because this appeared to violate locality; Bell's theorem and later experiments confirmed entanglement is real.

40What is the Schrödinger equation and what does its wave function (Ψ) represent in chemistry?

Answer

The Schrödinger equation describes how quantum states evolve over time. In chemistry, Ψ² gives the probability density of finding an electron at a given location — defining atomic orbitals and electron cloud shapes.

41What is the endosymbiotic theory and what evidence supports it?

Answer

The theory (proposed by Lynn Margulis) holds that mitochondria and chloroplasts were once free-living prokaryotes engulfed by proto-eukaryotic cells. Evidence: they have double membranes, their own circular DNA, ribosomes resembling bacterial ones, and they divide by binary fission.

42What is the Standard Model of particle physics and what particles does it include?

Answer

The Standard Model describes fundamental particles and forces. It includes fermions (quarks: up/down/charm/strange/top/bottom; leptons: electron/muon/tau + their neutrinos) and bosons mediating forces (photon, W/Z bosons, gluon, Higgs boson). Gravity is not included.

43What is chirality in organic chemistry and why does it matter in pharmacology?

Answer

A chiral molecule has a non-superimposable mirror image (enantiomers). In pharmacology, enantiomers can have vastly different biological activities: e.g., (S)-ibuprofen is the active form, while (R)-ibuprofen is far less effective. Thalidomide's tragedy involved one enantiomer being therapeutic and the other teratogenic.

44What is the role of telomeres in aging and cancer?

Answer

Telomeres are repetitive DNA caps protecting chromosome ends. They shorten with each cell division; when critically short, cells enter senescence or apoptosis. Cancer cells often upregulate telomerase to maintain telomere length and achieve replicative immortality.

45What is the difference between general and special relativity?

Answer

Special relativity (1905) addresses inertial (non-accelerating) frames: time dilation, length contraction, mass-energy equivalence at constant velocity. General relativity (1915) extends this to accelerating frames and gravity, describing gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass-energy.

46What is a Grignard reagent and how is it used in organic synthesis?

Answer

A Grignard reagent is an organomagnesium halide (RMgX) formed by reacting an alkyl or aryl halide with magnesium in dry ether. As a strong nucleophile/base, it attacks carbonyl groups (aldehydes, ketones, esters) to form new C–C bonds, enabling construction of complex alcohols.

47What is apoptosis and how does the intrinsic pathway trigger it?

Answer

Apoptosis is programmed cell death. The intrinsic pathway is triggered by cellular stress (DNA damage, oxidative stress) → pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 proteins (Bax, Bak) overcome anti-apoptotic ones (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL) → cytochrome c released from mitochondria → apoptosome complex → caspase-9 activation → effector caspases (3, 7) → cell dismantled without inflammation.

48What is a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) and under what conditions does it form?

Answer

A BEC is a state of matter formed when bosons (integer-spin particles) are cooled to near absolute zero, causing a macroscopic fraction to occupy the same lowest quantum ground state, behaving as a single quantum entity. First created experimentally in 1995 (Cornell & Wieman).

49What is the difference between SN1 and SN2 nucleophilic substitution mechanisms?

Answer

SN2 is a concerted one-step reaction (backside attack by nucleophile as leaving group departs) favoured by primary substrates and strong nucleophiles; configuration is inverted (Walden inversion). SN1 proceeds in two steps (rate-determining ionisation forming carbocation, then nucleophile attacks) favoured by tertiary substrates and polar protic solvents; gives racemic mixture.

50What is synthetic biology and what are its key applications and ethical concerns?

Answer

Synthetic biology applies engineering principles to design novel biological systems: examples include engineered bacteria producing insulin, biosensors, cell-free diagnostics, and minimal cells. Ethical concerns: biosafety (ecological release), biosecurity (bioweapons), equitable access, and the moral status of engineered life.

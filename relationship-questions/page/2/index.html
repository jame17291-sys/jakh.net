21What is 'repair attempt' in Gottman's research and why is it important?

Answer

A repair attempt is any action or statement during conflict that de-escalates tension — a joke, an apology, or touching the partner's hand. Couples who successfully use and accept repair attempts are far more likely to have stable, satisfying relationships.

22What is 'projection' in the context of relationships?

Answer

A defence mechanism where a person attributes their own unwanted feelings or traits onto their partner — e.g., someone who is angry accuses their partner of being angry. It deflects self-awareness and can create false conflict.

23What is the 'Michelangelo phenomenon' in relationship psychology?

Answer

The process by which close partners 'sculpt' each other toward their ideal selves through behavioural confirmation. Partners who affirm each other's ideal self-perceptions promote greater well-being and relationship satisfaction over time.

24What neurochemical changes occur during the early stages of romantic love, and how do they differ from long-term attachment?

Answer

Early romantic love activates dopamine (reward/craving), norepinephrine (excitement), and serotonin drops (obsessive thinking). Long-term attachment shifts to oxytocin (bonding) and vasopressin (pair-bonding), producing calm, stable connection rather than intense craving.

25What is the difference between 'solvable' and 'perpetual' problems in relationships, according to Gottman?

Answer

Solvable problems have a clear resolution (logistics, chores). Perpetual problems (69% of all relationship conflicts) stem from fundamental personality differences or needs and never fully resolve — the goal is not to solve them but to manage them with humour, acceptance, and ongoing dialogue.

26What is 'anxious attachment' and what patterns of behaviour does it typically produce in adult romantic relationships?

Answer

Anxious attachment (preoccupied) produces hyperactivated attachment behaviour: constant reassurance-seeking, jealousy, fear of abandonment, difficulty tolerating partner independence, and protest behaviour (anger, crying) when separation is anticipated.

27What is 'stonewalling' and what does Gottman's research show about its physiological basis?

Answer

Stonewalling is emotional shutdown — the listener withdraws all engagement (blank face, monosyllabic responses, physical stillness). Gottman found it typically follows flooding and is more common in men. The stonewallers' heart rate often exceeds 100 bpm, indicating they are actually in physiological overwhelm, not indifference.

28What is 'compassion fatigue' and how does it affect caregiving relationships?

Answer

Compassion fatigue is emotional depletion from sustained exposure to others' suffering — common in caregivers and therapists. In intimate relationships, the caregiver partner may feel numbed, resentful, or burnt out, reducing their capacity for empathy. Self-care and professional support are critical mitigators.

29What is 'self-expansion theory' in romantic relationships?

Answer

Arthur Aron's theory that people are motivated to expand the self by including others' perspectives, resources, and identities. Romantic partners who continue to offer novel, challenging experiences maintain higher satisfaction because the self-expansion drive continues to be met.

30What is DARVO and in what type of relationship dynamic does it typically appear?

Answer

DARVO (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender) is a manipulation tactic: the person accused of harmful behaviour denies it, attacks the person raising the concern, and then claims to be the real victim. It is typical in abusive or narcissistically structured relationships.

31What is 'secure base' in attachment theory, and how does it function in adult romantic relationships?

Answer

Bowlby described the secure base as a trusted attachment figure from whom the individual can safely explore the world, knowing they can return for comfort. In adults, romantic partners serve as secure bases: felt security from the partner enables greater autonomy, risk-taking, and resilience.

32What is 'emotional regulation' and why is it important for relationship health?

Answer

The ability to manage and respond to emotional experiences in a flexible, adaptive way. Good emotional regulation prevents impulsive reactions during conflict, supports empathy, and promotes constructive communication. Poor regulation (over- or under-reactivity) is a major predictor of relationship dissatisfaction.

33What is 'meta-communication' and how is it used to improve relationship quality?

Answer

Communication about communication — discussing how partners talk to each other, not just the content. E.g., 'I notice we tend to raise our voices when we talk about finances. Can we agree on a calmer approach?' It helps couples create shared norms and identify problematic patterns before they escalate.

34What does research show about the role of gratitude in long-term relationship satisfaction?

Answer

Studies by Sara Algoe and others show that expressing genuine gratitude toward a partner ('find, remind, and bind') strengthens communal norms, increases felt appreciation, and predicts greater relationship satisfaction and commitment months later — even after controlling for positive affect.

35What is 'collaborative problem-solving' and how does it differ from 'win-lose' conflict resolution?

Answer

In win-lose, one person's goal is met at the other's expense. In collaborative problem-solving, both partners articulate their underlying needs and work together to find solutions that satisfy both — producing higher-quality decisions, preserving the relationship, and increasing trust.

36What is 'negative sentiment override' and how does it affect perception in troubled relationships?

Answer

Gottman's term for a state in distressed couples where even neutral or positive partner behaviours are perceived as negative. It reverses the 'positive sentiment override' of healthy couples and creates a self-reinforcing negative lens that makes repair very difficult.

37What is the 'closeness-communication bias' and why can it harm long-term relationships?

Answer

The tendency to assume close partners understand us better than they actually do — leading to less careful communication, omitted context, and untested assumptions. Research by Boaz Keysar found that partners are often no more accurate at understanding each other than strangers, making assumed communication dangerous.

38What is 'differentiation of self' in Bowen Family Systems Theory and why does it matter for intimacy?

Answer

Differentiation is the ability to maintain a stable sense of self while remaining emotionally connected to others. High differentiation allows genuine intimacy without fusion or distance; low differentiation leads to emotional fusion (losing the self in the relationship) or emotional cut-off.

39What is Mating Intelligence and how does it explain individual differences in relationship outcomes?

Answer

Mating intelligence (Geher & Miller) encompasses cognitive abilities related to navigating mating contexts: accurately assessing partner qualities, signalling one's own value, reading commitment cues, and detecting deception. Higher mating intelligence predicts better partner selection and longer relationship satisfaction, beyond general intelligence.

40What is the 'investment model' of relationship commitment (Rusbult) and what three factors predict commitment?

Answer

Rusbult's Investment Model predicts commitment through: (1) Satisfaction — rewards minus costs; (2) Investment size — resources poured into the relationship (time, shared friends, memories); (3) Quality of alternatives — the attractiveness of leaving. High satisfaction + high investment + poor alternatives = strong commitment and accommodation of partner transgressions.

41What does Terror Management Theory predict about the role of close relationships in mortality salience?

Answer

TMT (Greenberg, Pyszczynski, Solomon) holds that awareness of death creates existential anxiety. Close relationships serve as symbolic immortality buffers — they give life meaning and continuity. Under mortality salience, people become more protective of romantic partners who share worldviews and more hostile to those who challenge them.

42What is 'mentalization' (reflective functioning) and how does it protect against intergenerational transmission of attachment insecurity?

Answer

Mentalization is the ability to understand one's own and others' behaviour in terms of underlying mental states (intentions, feelings, desires). High reflective functioning (Fonagy) allows parents who had insecure childhoods to break the cycle by understanding their child's inner world, producing secure attachment in their children despite their own history.

43What is 'affective forecasting error' and how does it affect relationship decisions?

Answer

Affective forecasting error (Wilson & Gilbert) is the tendency to misjudge the intensity and duration of future emotional states. In relationships, people overestimate how happy they'll be after a positive event (e.g., marriage) and overestimate how devastated they'll be after a negative one (e.g., break-up), influencing commitment and exit decisions.

44What is the 'dark triad' and what does research show about dark triad individuals' mating strategies?

Answer

The dark triad comprises narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy. Research shows dark triad individuals favour short-term mating: they engage in more deceptive self-presentation, manipulate partner poaching, tend toward infidelity and commitment avoidance, and display charm in early stages that later gives way to exploitation.

45What is 'interpersonal complementarity' (Kiesler) and how does it shape relationship dynamics?

Answer

The principle that interpersonal behaviours pull for reciprocal responses: dominance pulls for submission, submission pulls for dominance (complementarity on control); affiliation pulls for affiliation (correspondence on warmth). These automatic pulls shape interaction patterns and can trap couples in rigid cycles (e.g., controlling partner + increasingly dependent partner).

46What is 'capitulation accommodation' versus 'exit-voice-loyalty-neglect' in relationship conflict, and what predicts which pathway is chosen?

Answer

Rusbult's EVLN model describes responses to dissatisfaction: Exit (leave), Voice (discuss problem), Loyalty (wait hoping things improve), Neglect (passive deterioration). High commitment + positive sentiment override predicts constructive responses (Voice, Loyalty). Low investment + poor relationship quality predicts destructive ones (Exit, Neglect). Capitulation (immediate compliance) can be either accommodative or defensive depending on motivation.

47What is the 'actor-partner interdependence model' (APIM) and why is it important in relationship research?

Answer

APIM (Kenny & Judd) is a statistical framework for dyadic data that simultaneously estimates actor effects (how one's own variable predicts one's outcome) and partner effects (how one partner's variable predicts the other's outcome). It is essential because standard independent-sample analyses assume non-independence, which is violated when both members of a couple are studied.

48What is 'perceived partner responsiveness' and what role does it play as a foundation of intimacy?

Answer

Perceived partner responsiveness (Reis & Shaver) is the perception that a partner understands, validates, and cares for the self — comprising understanding, validation, and caring. It is considered the core ingredient of intimacy and predicts relationship satisfaction, trust, commitment, and personal well-being across cultures.

49What is the 'abstinence violation effect' in the context of relationship relapse after separation?

Answer

Originally from addiction research (Marlatt), the abstinence violation effect describes how one slip (e.g., contacting an ex) leads to catastrophic thinking ('I've already failed') and complete relapse into the relationship. Applied to post-separation behaviour, it explains why a single moment of contact often triggers full reconciliation or destructive re-engagement cycles.

50What is 'communal versus exchange orientation' in relationships and when does each become appropriate?

Answer

Clark & Mills: in communal relationships (close friends, romantic partners), people give benefits in response to need without expecting direct repayment — tracking debts signals distrust. In exchange relationships (business partners, acquaintances), people expect equivalent return for benefits given. Applying exchange norms to communal relationships lowers satisfaction and signals low commitment.

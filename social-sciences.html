👥 Social Sciences Easy Sociology

Who is considered the founder of sociology?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

Auguste Comte, who coined the term 'sociology' in the 19th century.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Economics

What is GDP (Gross Domestic Product)?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The total monetary value of all goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Political Science

What is the difference between a democracy and an autocracy?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

In a democracy, power is held by the people (directly or through elected representatives). In an autocracy, power is concentrated in a single ruler with little or no accountability.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Anthropology

What is culture in anthropological terms?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The shared beliefs, values, norms, practices, symbols, and knowledge transmitted across generations within a human group.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Sociology

What is social stratification?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The hierarchical arrangement of individuals or groups in society based on wealth, power, prestige, or other criteria.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Economics

What is inflation?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing the purchasing power of money.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Political Science

What is the separation of powers in government?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The division of government authority into three branches — legislative (makes laws), executive (enforces laws), and judicial (interprets laws) — to prevent any one branch from becoming too powerful.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Anthropology

What is ethnography?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

A qualitative research method in which an anthropologist immerses themselves in a community over an extended period to observe and document its way of life.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Sociology

What does the term 'social norms' mean?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

Shared rules and expectations within a group or society that guide behaviour — e.g., shaking hands when greeting, queuing in line.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Economics

What is supply and demand?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

Supply is the amount of a product producers are willing to sell at a given price; demand is the amount consumers want to buy. Where they meet determines the market price.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Political Science

What is the United Nations and when was it founded?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

An international organisation founded in 1945 after World War II to promote peace, security, and international cooperation among its member states.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Sociology

What is socialisation in sociology?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The lifelong process by which individuals learn and internalise the values, norms, and behaviours appropriate to their culture and social positions.

👥 Social Sciences Easy Anthropology

What is cultural relativism?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The principle of understanding a culture on its own terms rather than judging it by the standards of another culture.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Sociology

What is Max Weber's concept of 'ideal types' and how is it used in sociological analysis?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

An ideal type is an analytical construct that exaggerates or emphasises certain characteristics of a social phenomenon to create a benchmark for comparison — e.g., 'bureaucracy' as a pure rational-legal form. It doesn't describe reality but helps identify deviations from it.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Economics

What is the difference between microeconomics and macroeconomics?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

Microeconomics studies individual economic agents (firms, households) and their decisions. Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole — national output, unemployment, inflation, monetary policy.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Political Science

What is the social contract theory, and how do Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau differ on it?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

Social contract theory holds that political authority derives from agreement among people. Hobbes: without authority, life is 'nasty, brutish, short' — people surrender rights to a sovereign. Locke: government's legitimacy requires protecting natural rights (life, liberty, property); people can revolt if it fails. Rousseau: the general will of the community is the true sovereign.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Sociology

What is Émile Durkheim's concept of 'anomie'?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

A state of normlessness or breakdown in social norms, caused by rapid social change, where individuals feel disconnected from their society's values. Durkheim linked anomie to higher suicide rates in his 1897 study.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Anthropology

What is the 'Sapir-Whorf hypothesis' (linguistic relativity)?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The idea that the language one speaks influences or determines how they perceive and think about the world. The strong version claims language determines thought; the weak version claims language influences thought. Experimental evidence supports the weak version.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Economics

What is the 'invisible hand' concept in Adam Smith's economics?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

The idea that individuals pursuing their own self-interest in a free market unintentionally promote the overall economic welfare of society — as if guided by an invisible hand. It is a key argument for market economies over central planning.

👥 Social Sciences Medium Political Science

What is the difference between a federal and a unitary state?

◷ Editorial fact review pending

In a federal state, power is divided between a central government and regional/state governments (e.g., USA, Germany). In a unitary state, the central government holds supreme authority and sub-national governments operate under its delegation (e.g., France, UK).

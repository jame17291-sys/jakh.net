🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Greetings & Gestures

In Japan, bowing is the traditional greeting. What does a deeper bow generally indicate?

Greater respect or formality — a shallow bow (15°) is casual, a 30° bow shows respect, and a 45° bow indicates deep apology or reverence.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Dining Etiquette

In China, what does tapping two fingers on the table during tea service mean?

It is a sign of thanks to the person pouring tea — originating from a story about an emperor who bowed anonymously; his companions tapped fingers to symbolise bowing without revealing his identity.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Social Customs

In many Middle Eastern and South Asian cultures, why is it considered rude to offer something with the left hand?

Traditionally, the left hand is considered unclean because it is associated with hygiene tasks. Gifts, food, and greetings should always be given and received with the right hand.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Dining Etiquette

In France, what is considered impolite about asking for a 'doggy bag' at a restaurant?

It was long considered an insult to the chef — implying the portions were too large (over-served) or that the diner did not appreciate the food enough to eat it there. In recent years, France passed laws encouraging restaurants to offer take-home boxes to reduce food waste.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Greetings & Gestures

In New Zealand, what is the name of the traditional Māori greeting performed by pressing noses and foreheads?

The hongi — it symbolises the sharing of the breath of life (ha) between two people.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Social Customs

In Thailand, why is it extremely disrespectful to touch someone on the head?

In Thai Buddhist belief, the head is the most sacred part of the body — the highest point — and is considered spiritually pure. The feet are the lowest and least sacred, so pointing feet at people or sacred objects is also rude.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Dining Etiquette

In Japan, what is the etiquette rule about passing food from chopstick to chopstick?

It is strictly forbidden — it mimics the ritual of passing cremated bones between family members at a funeral. Food should be placed on a plate before others pick it up.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Religious & Ceremonial

When visiting a mosque, what must both men and women do before entering the prayer hall?

Remove their shoes. Visitors must also dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees at minimum), and women are typically expected to cover their hair.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Social Customs

In Brazil, what does the 'OK' hand gesture (circle formed by thumb and index finger) mean?

It is a highly offensive obscene gesture — roughly equivalent to the middle finger in North American culture.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Greetings & Gestures

In many European countries (France, Spain, Italy), what is the typical greeting between people who know each other?

Cheek kisses (la bise). The number of kisses varies: France is typically 2 (or 1-4 by region), Spain 2, Italy 2.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Dining Etiquette

In China, is it polite or impolite to finish all the food on your plate?

Traditionally impolite — finishing everything can signal to the host that they did not provide enough food. Leaving a little shows you are full and satisfied.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Religious & Ceremonial

In India, during which festival do people throw coloured powder and water on each other?

Holi — the festival of colours, celebrated at the end of winter (usually March).

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Social Customs

In Germany, what is considered very rude to do before everyone has their drink at a table and before the toast?

Drinking before the host or before making eye contact during the toast (Prost!). German tradition also holds that failing to make eye contact during the clink brings seven years of bad luck.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Easy Dining Etiquette

In Ethiopia, what is 'gursha' and what does it represent?

Gursha is the act of feeding a bite of food directly into another person's mouth — a gesture of love, respect, and deep friendship. Refusing it is considered an insult.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Medium Social Customs

In Japan, what is the concept of 'tatemae' and 'honne' and how does it shape social interaction?

Tatemae is the public face — what one presents to maintain group harmony and social expectations. Honne is the true inner feelings. Japanese social interaction often requires suppressing honne in favour of tatemae, avoiding direct confrontation or negative responses.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Medium Dining Etiquette

In South Korea, what is the etiquette rule about who should pour drinks at the table?

You should never pour your own drink — others at the table pour for you, and you pour for them. It reflects Confucian respect for hierarchy and communal care. The eldest or most senior person is typically served first.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Medium Religious & Ceremonial

In Mexico, what is the Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos) and how does it differ from a typical mourning tradition?

Celebrated November 1–2, it honours deceased loved ones with altars (ofrendas), marigolds, sugar skulls, and their favourite food and objects. Unlike solemn mourning, it is joyful — the belief is that the dead return to visit the living during this window.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Medium Greetings & Gestures

In which country does nodding the head up and down mean 'no' rather than 'yes'?

Bulgaria (and parts of Greece). A head toss upward means 'no', and a side-to-side shake can mean 'yes' — the opposite of most Western conventions.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Medium Social Customs

What is the Finnish concept of 'talkoot' and how does it reflect Finnish cultural values?

Talkoot is the tradition of communal work — neighbours and community members gathering to help each other with large tasks (harvesting, building). It reflects Finland's deeply communal, egalitarian values and practical mutual-aid ethic.

🌍 World Habits & Etiquette Medium Dining Etiquette

In India, why do many Hindus avoid eating beef and why do many Muslims avoid pork?

Hinduism venerates the cow as sacred — a symbol of motherhood, abundance, and non-violence (ahimsa). Islam prohibits pork as haram (forbidden) based on Quranic dietary laws.
